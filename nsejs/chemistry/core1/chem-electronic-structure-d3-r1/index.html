Core1A — taught operation

Deconstructing bulk principal quantum shell counts (M=9, N=2) into specific orbital occupancies (3s2 3p6 3d1 4s2), distinguishing core vs valence electrons, counting unpaired spins, and identifying d-block transition identity.

Justified steps

  1. RETRIEVE — Sum electrons across shells K + L + M + N = Z and identify the neutral atomic element.. Why valid: Total electrons in a neutral atom equal nuclear charge Z.
  2. REPRESENT — Decompose shell populations into subshells following (n+l) Aufbau ordering.. Why valid: M-shell (n=3) has 3s(2) + 3p(6) + 3d(1) = 9, and N-shell (n=4) has 4s(2).
  3. SYNTHESIZE — Enumerate specific electron categories: total p-electrons, total d-electrons, and unpaired spins.. Why valid: Fully filled subshells 1s2, 2s2, 2p6, 3s2, 3p6, 4s2 pair completely, leaving 3d1 as the sole unpaired electron.
  4. JUSTIFY — Classify periodic block based on the subshell receiving the differentiating electron.. Why valid: Last electron enters 3d, which defines the element as a d-block transition metal, refuting p-block classification.

An atom of Chromium (Cr, Z=24) has shell population K = 2, L = 8, M = 13, N = 1. Write its full subshell configuration, determine total p-electrons, total d-electrons, unpaired electrons, and periodic block.

  1. 1. Atomic number: Z = 2 + 8 + 13 + 1 = 24 (Chromium, Cr).
  2. 2. Subshell configuration: Due to half-filled subshell stability, Cr is 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p6 3d5 4s1.
  3. 3. Electron counts: p-electrons = 2p6 + 3p6 = 12; d-electrons = 3d5 = 5; unpaired electrons = 5 (in 3d) + 1 (in 4s) = 6.
  4. 4. Block classification: Differentiating electrons enter 3d, placing Cr in the d-block.

Misconceptions

  • —
  • Because the 3d electron filled after 4s, it must always leave before 4s in a transition-metal cation. — Sc first fills [Ar]3d1 4s2, but the 4s electrons are removed before 3d during common Sc cation formation; compare Sc2+ [Ar]3d1 with Sc3+ [Ar].
