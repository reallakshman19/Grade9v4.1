Inorganic Chemistry — Phosphorus Oxyacids Structure & Basicity

Target Item: NSEJS-2024-C-Q55 | Band: D3 (Score 6) | Operation: SYNTHESIZE

NSEJS 2024 Reference Item

Problem Statement

[Multiple Correct] Exactly 1.0 mol of a phosphorus oxyacid H3POx is completely neutralised by 40.0 g of sodium hydroxide (NaOH, M = 40.0 g mol-1). Which of the following statements are correct?

  • (A) x = 2 and the acid is monobasic.
  • (B) x = 3 and the acid is dibasic.
  • (C) The acid contains two ionisable P-OH bonds.
  • (D) The acid acts as a powerful reducing agent.

Select Correct Statements:


Inorganic Chemistry Derivation & Proof

1. System Representation & Setup: Moles of NaOH = 40.0 g / 40.0 g mol-1 = 1.0 mol. Complete neutralisation of 1.0 mol acid requires 1.0 mol monoacidic base.

2. Physical Model & Formulation: Basicity = n(NaOH) / n(Acid) = 1.0 / 1.0 = 1. In phosphorus oxyacids H3POx, basicity equals the number of P-OH bonds: Basicity = x - 1 = 1 ⇒ x = 2. The formula is H3PO2 (hypophosphorous acid).

3. Execution & Resolution: H3PO2 contains 1 P=O bond, 1 ionisable P-OH bond, and 2 non-ionisable P-H bonds. Because it possesses two direct P-H hydride bonds, it acts as a strong reducing agent (e.g. reducing Ag+ to metallic Ag). Therefore, Statements A and D are verified true, while B and C are false.

4. Limiting Check: H3PO3 requires 2 mol NaOH (dibasic), and H3PO4 requires 3 mol NaOH (tribasic).

Core 1A: Declarative Teaching Construction

Prerequisite

P-OH versus P-H Bond Polarity

Only protons attached to oxygen (P-OH) are acidic because the high electronegativity of oxygen polarizes the bond. Direct P-H bonds are non-polar (EN of P = 2.19, H = 2.20) and do not ionize, but act as reducing centers.

Worked Example

Phosphorous Acid H3PO3 Neutralization

0.50 mol of H3PO3 requires 1.0 mol of NaOH (Basicity = 2). It has two P-OH bonds and one reducing P-H bond.

Independent Exit Check

Pyrophosphoric Acid H4P2O7 Check

No P-H bonds ⇒ Basicity = 4. 0.25 mol requires 1.0 mol NaOH (40.0 g).
