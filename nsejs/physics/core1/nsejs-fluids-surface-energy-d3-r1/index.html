Fluid Mechanics — Droplet Coalescence Thermodynamics

Target Item: PHYS-NSEJS-FLUID-04 | Band: D3 (Score 6) | Operation: SYNTHESIZE

Olympiad High-Demand Problem

Problem Statement

N = 1000 identical spherical water microdroplets, each of radius r = 1.0 × 10-4 m and surface tension T = 0.075 N/m, coalesce under isothermal conditions to form a single large spherical drop. Calculate the total surface energy released (ΔU) in this coalescence process.

Select Option:


Mathematical Physics Derivation & Proof

1. System Representation & Setup: N = 1000 droplets of radius r = 1.0 × 10-4 m and surface tension T = 0.075 N/m coalesce into a single drop of radius R.

2. Physical Model & Formulation: Incompressible volume conservation: (4/3)πR3 = 1000 × (4/3)πr3 ⇒ R = 10001/3 r = 10r = 1.0 × 10-3 m. Initial area Ai = 1000 × 4πr2. Final area Af = 4π(10r)2 = 100 × 4πr2. Net surface area reduction: ΔA = Ai - Af = (1000 - 100) × 4πr2 = 3600πr2.

3. Execution & Resolution: ΔA = 3600 × π × (1.0 × 10-4)2 = 3.6π × 10-5 m2 = 1.13097 × 10-4 m2. Released surface energy: ΔU = T × ΔA = 0.075 × 1.13097 × 10-4 = 8.482 × 10-6 J ≈ 8.48 × 10-6 J. Matching Option A.

4. Limiting Check: As N → ∞, fraction of released surface energy ΔU / Ui = 1 - N-1/3 → 1 (100%).

Core 1A: Declarative Teaching Construction

Prerequisite

Non-Linear Geometric Scaling (r³ vs r²)

Volume scales as the cube of the radius, while surface area scales as the square. Conserving volume while coalescing spheres drastically compresses the surface area, releasing surface potential energy.

Worked Example

Eight Mercury Droplets Coalescence

N = 8 droplets, r = 2.0 mm, T = 0.485 N/m. R = 81/3 r = 2r = 4.0 mm. ΔA = 4πr2(8 - 4) = 16πr2 = 2.01 × 10-4 m2. ΔU = 0.485 × 2.01 × 10-4 = 9.75 × 10-5 J.

Independent Exit Check

64 Water Droplets Coalescence Check

Fraction of surface area eliminated = 1 - 64-1/3 = 1 - 1/4 = 3/4 (75%).
