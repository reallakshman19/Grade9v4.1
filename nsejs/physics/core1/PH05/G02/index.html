1 · Predict before solving

If both the mass and radius of a planet are halved, does its surface gravity double, halve, or stay the same?

2 · Attempt: protect your decisive work

An exoplanet has mass M_p = 0.5 M_E and radius R_p = 0.5 R_E. An astronaut who can jump vertically to a maximum height of 0.80 m on Earth jumps on this exoplanet with the identical takeoff velocity. What maximum height does the astronaut reach on the exoplanet?

Consult this subtopic’s Core1A textbook (help exposure tracked)

H1–H3 · Optional ordered support

Only after the first answer has been saved. Supports may orient, but do not count as independent first attempts.

First compute the exoplanet's surface gravity g_p in terms of Earth's g_E using g = G * M / R^2.

Notice that takeoff velocity v is identical on both planets.

Use vertical jump kinematics: v^2 = 2 * g * h ==> h is inversely proportional to g.

3 · Reconstruct after attempting

Shortest defensible route

1. Surface gravity on exoplanet: g_p = G * (0.5 M_E) / (0.5 R_E)^2 = (0.5 / 0.25) * (G * M_E / R_E^2) = 2.0 * g_E. 2. Jump height relation: Vertical kinematics under constant gravity gives v^2 = 2 * g * h ==> h = v^2 / (2g). For identical takeoff speed v, h is inversely proportional to g: h_p / h_E = g_E / g_p = 1 / 2. 3. Evaluation: h_p = 0.80 m / 2 = 0.40 m. The astronaut jumps only half as high because surface gravity is doubled.

Independent route/check

Work-energy equivalence: The astronaut's leg muscles do work W = F_leg * d_leg = m * g * h. For equal work output W, h = W / (m * g) ==> h_p / h_E = g_E / g_p = 1/2 ==> h_p = 0.40 m.

4 · Diagnose the first divergence

If you answered 1.60 m, you inverted the relationship between gravity and jump height (higher gravity reduces jump height). If you answered 0.80 m, you assumed g was unchanged.

5 · Repair that step

Remember that halving radius quarters the denominator (1/0.25 = 4), which overwhelms halving the mass (0.5 * 4 = 2). Double gravity cuts vertical jump height in half.

6 · Boundary: new changed condition

What jump height would the astronaut achieve on a small asteroid where mass is 10^-6 M_E and radius is 10^-3 R_E?

g_ast = (10^-6) / (10^-3)^2 * g_E = 1.0 * g_E. Even though the asteroid is tiny, the ratio M/R^2 yields exactly 1 g_E, so jump height remains 0.80 m.
