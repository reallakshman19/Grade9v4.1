1 · Predict before solving

If a satellite in orbit r loses 10 MJ of total mechanical energy due to atmospheric drag, does its kinetic energy increase by 10 MJ, decrease by 10 MJ, or stay constant?

2 · Attempt: protect your decisive work

A 1000 kg scientific satellite revolves in a circular low Earth orbit. Due to residual atmospheric drag over a period of 6 months, the satellite's orbital altitude drops slightly, causing a total mechanical energy loss of Delta_E = -200 MJ. (1) How does the satellite's kinetic energy change? (2) How does its potential energy change? (3) Does its orbital speed increase or decrease?

H1–H3 · Optional ordered support

Only after the first answer has been saved. Supports may orient, but do not count as independent first attempts.

Write the relationships between E, K, and U for a circular orbit: K = -E and U = 2E.

Differentiate these relationships with respect to energy changes: Delta_K = -Delta_E.

Check whether kinetic energy increase implies an increase in speed: K = 0.5 * m * v^2.

3 · Reconstruct after attempting

Shortest defensible route

1. Kinetic Energy Change: For any bound circular orbit, total energy E = -K. Therefore, Delta_K = -Delta_E. Since Delta_E = -200 MJ: Delta_K = -(-200 MJ) = +200 MJ. Kinetic energy increases by 200 MJ! 2. Potential Energy Change: Total energy relation U = 2 * E ==> Delta_U = 2 * Delta_E = 2 * (-200 MJ) = -400 MJ. Potential energy decreases by 400 MJ. 3. Energy Check: Delta_E = Delta_K + Delta_U = +200 MJ + (-400 MJ) = -200 MJ (perfectly consistent). 4. Speed Direction: Because Delta_K > 0 and K = 0.5 * m * v^2, the satellite's orbital speed increases.

Independent route/check

Use orbital speed formula: v = sqrt(GM/r). As altitude drops, r decreases. Smaller denominator in sqrt(GM/r) directly forces speed v to be larger.

4 · Diagnose the first divergence

If you answered that kinetic energy decreased by 200 MJ, you assumed friction must always reduce speed. In gravity orbits, potential energy loss exceeds friction loss by a factor of 2, funneling excess energy into speed.

5 · Repair that step

Memorize the virial ratio: for every 1 Joule of energy lost to friction, potential energy drops by 2 Joules, and kinetic energy gains 1 Joule.

6 · Boundary: new changed condition

What happens when the satellite enters dense atmosphere where drag exceeds gravity's ability to maintain circular orbit?

When drag becomes dominant, the orbit ceases to be circular. The virial equilibrium breaks down, drag deceleration overwhelms gravitational acceleration, and terminal deceleration causes the satellite to burn up.
