1 · Predict before solving

If two isolated masses of unequal mass attract each other from rest, will their kinetic energies at collision be equal or unequal?

2 · Attempt: protect your decisive work

Two isolated spherical probes of masses m1 = 200 kg and m2 = 800 kg are released from rest in deep space separated by center-to-center distance d = 100 m. When their separation has decreased to 50 m, determine the ratio of their momentum magnitudes p1/p2 and the ratio of their kinetic energies K1/K2.

Consult this subtopic’s Core1A textbook (help exposure tracked)

H1–H3 · Optional ordered support

Only after the first answer has been saved. Supports may orient, but do not count as independent first attempts.

Check whether any external force acts on the two-probe system.

Use total momentum conservation starting from rest: p1 + p2 = 0.

Relate kinetic energy directly to momentum magnitude: K = p^2 / (2m).

3 · Reconstruct after attempting

Shortest defensible route

1. System Isolation: Mutual gravity is internal to the two-probe system; F_ext = 0 ==> Total momentum is conserved: p1 + p2 = 0 ==> |p1| = |p2|. Thus ratio p1/p2 = 1 : 1. 2. Kinetic Energy Ratio: Using K = p^2 / (2m), K1/K2 = (p1^2 / 2m1) / (p2^2 / 2m2) = m2 / m1 = 800 / 200 = 4 : 1. The lighter probe acquires 4 times the kinetic energy of the heavier probe.

Independent route/check

Use accelerations: a1/a2 = (F/m1)/(F/m2) = m2/m1 = 4. Since both travel for the same duration t, v1/v2 = 4 ==> K1/K2 = (m1 * v1^2) / (m2 * v2^2) = (1/4) * 4^2 = 4.

4 · Diagnose the first divergence

If you answered that the heavier probe has more kinetic energy, ask whether you computed K = p^2/(2m) or assumed equal speeds. If you answered that forces were unequal, review Newton's third law.

5 · Repair that step

Recall that equal force over equal time delivers equal impulse and momentum; equal momentum into a smaller mass yields higher speed and greater kinetic energy.

6 · Boundary: new changed condition

What happens to the momentum ratio and kinetic energy ratio if the two probes have equal masses m1 = m2?

If m1 = m2, momentum magnitudes remain equal (|p1| = |p2|), and kinetic energies become exactly equal: K1/K2 = m2/m1 = 1 : 1.
