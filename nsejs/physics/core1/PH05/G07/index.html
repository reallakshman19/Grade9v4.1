1 · Predict before solving

If a particle is launched with speed v = v_e from a satellite orbit of radius r = 2R, does it escape, and with what residual speed?

2 · Attempt: protect your decisive work

A space probe is deployed from a circular parking orbit around Earth at radius r = 2 R_E. The probe fires its engine to achieve a speed v = v_e_surface (where v_e_surface = sqrt(2 G M_E / R_E) ≈ 11.2 km/s is the escape speed from Earth's surface). Determine the probe's speed v_inf when it has escaped to an infinite distance from Earth. Express your answer in terms of v_e_surface.

H1–H3 · Optional ordered support

Only after the first answer has been saved. Supports may orient, but do not count as independent first attempts.

State total mechanical energy at initial radius r = 2 R_E: E = 0.5 * m * v^2 - G * M_E * m / (2 R_E).

Express gravitational potential energy at 2 R_E in terms of surface escape speed: G * M_E / R_E = 0.5 * v_e_surface^2.

Equate initial energy to kinetic energy at infinity: E = 0.5 * m * v_inf^2.

3 · Reconstruct after attempting

Shortest defensible route

1. Initial Energy Setup: At r = 2 R_E with launch speed v = v_e_surface: E_initial = (1/2)*m*v_e^2 - G*M_E*m / (2 R_E). 2. Express GM_E / R_E in terms of v_e: Surface escape speed is v_e = sqrt(2*G*M_E / R_E) ==> G*M_E / R_E = (1/2)*v_e^2. Therefore, potential energy at 2 R_E is: U = - (1/2)*[G*M_E*m / R_E] = - (1/2)*[(1/2)*m*v_e^2] = - (1/4)*m*v_e^2. 3. Total Initial Energy: E_initial = (1/2)*m*v_e^2 - (1/4)*m*v_e^2 = (1/4)*m*v_e^2. 4. Energy at Infinity: At r -> infinity, U_inf = 0 ==> E_inf = (1/2)*m*v_inf^2. Equating energies: (1/2)*m*v_inf^2 = (1/4)*m*v_e^2 ==> v_inf^2 = (1/2)*v_e^2 ==> v_inf = v_e / sqrt(2) ≈ 0.707 * v_e_surface.

Independent route/check

Since escape speed from 2 R_E is v_e(2R) = sqrt(2*GM / 2R) = v_e / sqrt(2), launch speed v = v_e exceeds local escape speed. Energy conservation gives v_inf^2 = v^2 - v_e(2R)^2 = v_e^2 - (v_e^2 / 2) = v_e^2 / 2 ==> v_inf = v_e / sqrt(2).

4 · Diagnose the first divergence

If you answered v_inf = 0, you confused launching from Earth's surface with launching from an elevated orbit. At 2 R_E, gravity well is only half as deep, so the probe retains substantial kinetic energy.

5 · Repair that step

Notice that local escape speed decreases as 1/sqrt(r). Launching with full surface escape speed from an orbital altitude leaves excess kinetic energy that converts into speed at infinity.

6 · Boundary: new changed condition

What would v_inf be if the probe was launched from r = R_E with speed v = v_e_surface?

At the surface r = R_E, launch speed v = v_e exactly matches local escape speed, so E_initial = 0 and residual speed at infinity is v_inf = 0.
