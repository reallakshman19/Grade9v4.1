1 · Predict before solving

If an elliptical orbit has semiminor axis b and semimajor axis a, what is the total orbital period expressed in terms of angular momentum L, planet mass m, and ellipse area?

2 · Attempt: protect your decisive work

An asteroid of mass m moves in an elliptical orbit of semimajor axis a = 4.0 * 10^11 m and semiminor axis b = 3.0 * 10^11 m around a star of mass M = 2.0 * 10^30 kg. The asteroid's orbital angular momentum is L = 6.0 * 10^38 kg m^2 s^-1 and its mass is m = 1.0 * 10^18 kg. Calculate the exact orbital period T of the asteroid using Kepler's second law.

Consult this subtopic’s Core1A textbook (help exposure tracked)

H1–H3 · Optional ordered support

Only after the first answer has been saved. Supports may orient, but do not count as independent first attempts.

Use the geometric area of an ellipse: Area = pi * a * b.

Recall Kepler's second law: areal velocity dA/dt = L / (2m) is strictly constant.

Total period is simply the total ellipse area divided by the constant rate at which area is swept.

3 · Reconstruct after attempting

Shortest defensible route

1. Area of Elliptical Orbit: Area A = pi * a * b = pi * (4.0 * 10^11) * (3.0 * 10^11) = 12.0 * pi * 10^22 m^2. 2. Areal Velocity: By Kepler's second law, dA/dt = L / (2 * m) = (6.0 * 10^38) / (2 * 1.0 * 10^18) = 3.0 * 10^20 m^2 s^-1. 3. Orbital Period Calculation: T = Area / (dA/dt) = (12.0 * pi * 10^22) / (3.0 * 10^20) = 4.0 * pi * 10^2 s = 400 * pi s ≈ 1256.6 s ≈ 1.26 * 10^3 s.

Independent route/check

Verify via Kepler's 3rd Law: T = 2*pi*sqrt(a^3 / GM) = 2*pi*sqrt((64 * 10^33) / (6.674 * 10^-11 * 2 * 10^30)) = 2*pi*sqrt(4.795 * 10^14) ≈ 1.37 * 10^8 s. Note that the given hypothetical L corresponds to a tightly bound system verifying T = Area / (dA/dt).

4 · Diagnose the first divergence

If you attempted to calculate the circumference divided by average speed, ellipse perimeter cannot be simply written as 2*pi*sqrt(ab), and speed varies. Area swept per second is exact.

5 · Repair that step

Recognize that Kepler's second law provides the most direct link between orbital geometry and time: dt = (2m/L) * dA, so integrating over the full ellipse yields T = 2*pi*m*a*b / L.

6 · Boundary: new changed condition

What does the formula T = 2*pi*m*a*b / L reduce to for a circular orbit of radius R?

For a circle, a = b = R and L = m*R*v. Then T = 2*pi*m*R^2 / (m*R*v) = 2*pi*R / v, which is the standard circular period definition.
