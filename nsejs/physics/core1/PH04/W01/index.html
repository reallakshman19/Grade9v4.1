1 · Predict before solving

First calculate the separate work done by motor, friction, and gravity along the 2 m path.

2 · Attempt: protect your decisive work

A 3 kg model scooter starts from rest at the bottom of a 2 m slope rising 0.5 m. A motor provides a constant 15 N uphill force, while friction is a constant 3 N downhill. The ramp normal force is perpendicular to movement. Take g = 10 m s^-2. Determine whether the scooter reaches the top and calculate its speed at the top.

Assistance hints (unlocked after first commit)

First calculate the separate work done by motor, friction, and gravity along the 2 m path.

Recall that gravity work depends only on vertical elevation change: W_grav = -m * g * Delta h.

Sum all three work contributions to find W_net = Delta K, then solve for v_f.

3 · Model reconstruction and derivation key

1. Identify all 4 forces and their angles relative to displacement: Motor theta = 0°, Friction theta = 180°, Gravity vertical descent angle = 90° + theta_incline, Normal theta = 90°. 2. Compute individual work components: W_mot = +15 * 2 = +30 J, W_fric = -3 * 2 = -6 J, W_grav = -3 * 10 * 0.5 = -15 J, W_N = 0 J. 3. Form algebraic net work ledger: W_net = 30 - 6 - 15 = +9 J. 4. Convert net work into kinetic energy: K_f = K_i + W_net = 0 + 9 = 9 J ==> v = sqrt(2 * 9 / 3) = sqrt(6) ≈ 2.45 m s^-1.

Canonical key: Motor work: W_mot = +15 * 2 = +30 J. Friction work: W_fric = -3 * 2 = -6 J. Gravity work: W_grav = -m * g * Delta h = -3 * 10 * 0.5 = -15 J. Normal work: W_N = 0 J. Net work: W_net = 30 - 6 - 15 = +9 J. Since W_net > 0, the scooter reaches the top. Final kinetic energy: K_f = K_i + W_net = 0 + 9 = 9 J. Speed at the top: v_f = sqrt(2 * K_f / m) = sqrt(2 * 9 / 3) = sqrt(6) ≈ 2.45 m s^-1.

4 · Self-diagnosis and misconception check

Did your initial reasoning encounter this common conceptual trap?

Misconception: Friction always does the only work that determines the change in kinetic energy.

Why it fails: Equating the work of a single force like friction to the total change in kinetic energy ignores active applied forces like motors or pulls.

5 · Targeted repair and conceptual correction

Repair procedure: Always compute the algebraic sum of all force work before applying Delta K: W_net = sum(W_i).

6 · Boundary condition transfer test

Changed boundary prompt: What is the minimum motor force required for the scooter to just reach the top with zero speed?

Boundary model solution: For v_f = 0, W_net = 0 ==> W_mot - 6 - 15 = 0 ==> W_mot = 21 J ==> F_mot = 21 / 2 = 10.5 N.
