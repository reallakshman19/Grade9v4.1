2 · Attempt: protect your decisive work

A physics student analyzes three experimental cards with straight graph segments: Card R (force-position) has y-axis F_x (N) and x-axis x (m), rising linearly from (0 m, 0 N) to (4 m, 8 N). Assistance hints (unlocked after first commit)

Card R requires an area calculation (triangle: 0.5 * base * height).

Card S requires a slope calculation, with a negative sign because F_x = -dU/dx.

Card T requires the slope Delta s / Delta t over the interval from 2 s to 5 s.

3 · Model reconstruction and derivation key

1. Card R: Units of area = N * m = J ==> Area is Work. Triangular area = 0.5 * 4 * 8 = 16 J. 2. Card S: Units of slope = J / m = N ==> Slope relates to Force. By definition F_x = -dU/dx. Slope = (8 - 2)/(2 - 0) = +3 N ==> F_x = -3 N. 3. Card T: Units of slope = m / s ==> Slope is Velocity. v = (16 - 4)/(5 - 2) = 12 / 3 = 4 m/s. 4. Dimensional verification: [W] = 16 J, [F_x] = -3 N, [v] = 4 m/s.

Canonical key: 1. Card R (F_x vs x): Work is represented by the area under the triangular curve: W = 0.5 * base * height = 0.5 * (4 m) * (8 N) = +16 J. 2. Card S (U vs x): Slope of potential energy: dU/dx = (8 J - 2 J) / (2 m - 0 m) = 6 / 2 = +3 J m^-1. Conservative force is the negative gradient: F_x = -dU/dx = -3 N. 3. Card T (s vs t): Velocity between t = 2 s and t = 5 s is the slope: v = Delta s / Delta t = (16 m - 4 m) / (5 s - 2 s) = 12 / 3 = 4 m s^-1.

4 · Self-diagnosis and misconception check

Did your initial reasoning encounter this common conceptual trap?

Misconception: Area under any physics graph always represents work, and slope always represents acceleration.

Why it fails: Area under an s-t graph has units metre-seconds, which has no direct physical meaning. Area under U-x has units Joule-metres, not work.

5 · Targeted repair and conceptual correction

Repair procedure: Multiply axis units to check area: [N]*[m] = J (work). Divide axis units to check slope: [J]/[m] = N (force).

6 · Boundary condition transfer test

Changed boundary prompt: On Card S, what would the conservative force be if the potential energy curve flattened out horizontally between x = 2 m and x = 4 m at U = 8 J?

Boundary model solution: For a horizontal line, dU/dx = 0, so conservative force F_x = -dU/dx = 0 N.
