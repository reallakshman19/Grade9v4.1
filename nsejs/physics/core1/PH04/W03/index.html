2 · Attempt: protect your decisive work

A 3 kg trolley already rolls right at 2 m s^-1 on a smooth level track when a motor starts delivering a steady 18 W of mechanical power. Neglect friction and resistive losses. Calculate the trolley's speed after 2 seconds and after 6 seconds, and explain whether the acceleration remains constant.

Assistance hints (unlocked after first commit)

First calculate the initial kinetic energy K_0 before the motor starts.

Use K(t) = K_0 + P * t to find the total kinetic energy at t = 2 s and t = 6 s.

Remember that acceleration is a = P / (m * v); compare its value at 2 s and 6 s.

3 · Model reconstruction and derivation key

1. Compute initial kinetic energy: K_0 = 0.5 * m * v_0^2 = 0.5 * 3 * 4 = 6 J. 2. Apply constant power integral: Delta K(t) = P * t. 3. For t = 2 s: K(2) = 6 + 18*2 = 42 J ==> v(2) = sqrt(2 * 42 / 3) = sqrt(28) ≈ 5.29 m/s. 4. For t = 6 s: K(6) = 6 + 18*6 = 114 J ==> v(6) = sqrt(2 * 114 / 3) = sqrt(76) ≈ 8.72 m/s. 5. Evaluate acceleration: a(t) = P / (m * v(t)). Since v(t) increases, a(t) decreases continuously from 1.13 m/s^2 down to 0.69 m/s^2.

Canonical key: Initial kinetic energy: K_0 = 0.5 * 3 * (2)^2 = 6 J. 1. At t = 2 s: Added energy Delta K = P * t = 18 * 2 = 36 J. Total kinetic energy: K(2) = 6 + 36 = 42 J. Speed: v(2) = sqrt(2 * 42 / 3) = sqrt(28) ≈ 5.29 m s^-1. 2. At t = 6 s: Added energy Delta K = 18 * 6 = 108 J. Total kinetic energy: K(6) = 6 + 108 = 114 J. Speed: v(6) = sqrt(2 * 114 / 3) = sqrt(76) ≈ 8.72 m s^-1. 3. Acceleration: Instantaneous driving force F = P / v and acceleration a = P / (m * v). At t = 2 s, a = 18 / (3 * 5.29) ≈ 1.13 m s^-2. At t = 6 s, a = 18 / (3 * 8.72) ≈ 0.69 m s^-2. The acceleration decreases over time; it does not remain constant.

4 · Self-diagnosis and misconception check

Did your initial reasoning encounter this common conceptual trap?

Misconception: Constant power means constant force and constant acceleration.

Why it fails: Power is P = F * v. If power is constant and speed v increases, the driving force F = P/v must decrease proportionally.

5 · Targeted repair and conceptual correction

Repair procedure: Always use P = dK/dt to solve for energy first: K(t) = K_0 + P*t, then deduce v = sqrt(2K/m).

6 · Boundary condition transfer test

Changed boundary prompt: If the motor is suddenly switched off at t = 6 s and a braking force of 2 N is applied, how far does the trolley roll before stopping?

Boundary model solution: Stopping distance d = K(6) / F_brake = 114 / 2 = 57.0 m.
