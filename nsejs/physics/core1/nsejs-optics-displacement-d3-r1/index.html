Problem Statement

An illuminated object and a fixed viewing screen are placed on an optical bench at a fixed separation D = 90.0 cm. A thin convex lens of focal length f is placed between them. It is observed that sharp, real images of the object are formed on the screen at two distinct lens positions separated by a displacement d = 30.0 cm.

If h1 and h2 are the heights of the images formed at the two positions, and h0 is the height of the object:

Determine the focal length f of the convex lens and the ratio of image heights h1 / h2 (where h1 > h2).

Mathematical Physics Derivation & Invariant Proof

  1. 1. System Formulation & Spatial Constraints: Let the object be at origin x = 0 and fixed screen at x = D = 90.0 cm. Let u be the object distance from the lens, so image distance v = D - u. Substituting into the thin lens formula 1/v - 1/(-u) = 1/f ⇒ 1/(D - u) + 1/u = 1/f. Multiplying by u(D - u)f yields the standard quadratic equation:
    u² - Du + Df = 0
  2. 2. Conjugate Roots & Displacement Formula: The two real roots representing the two conjugate positions are:
    u₁ = (D - √(D² - 4Df))/2,   u₂ = (D + √(D² - 4Df))/2

    Their separation displacement is d = u2 - u1 = √(D2 - 4Df).
    Squaring both sides: d2 = D2 - 4Df ⇒ 4Df = D2 - d2.
    f = (D² - d²) / (4D) = (90.0² - 30.0²) / (4 × 90.0) = (8100 - 900) / 360 = 7200 / 360 = 20.0 cm
  3. 3. Image Magnifications & Height Ratio: At position 1: u1 = (90 - 30)/2 = 30.0 cm, so v1 = 90 - 30 = 60.0 cm.
    Magnification m1 = v1 / u1 = 60 / 30 = 2.0 ⇒ h1 = 2.0 h0.
    At position 2: u2 = (90 + 30)/2 = 60.0 cm, so v2 = 90 - 60 = 30.0 cm.
    Magnification m2 = v2 / u2 = 30 / 60 = 0.5 ⇒ h2 = 0.5 h0.
    The height ratio is therefore:
    h₁ / h₂ = 2.0 / 0.5 = 4.0

    Notice the invariant: m1 × m2 = 1.0, which implies h1 × h2 = h02 ⇒ h0 = √(h1 h2). Matches Option A.
  4. 4. Boundary Condition & Falsification Limits: For real images to form at two distinct positions on the screen, the quadratic discriminant must be strictly positive: Δ = D2 - 4Df > 0 ⇒ D > 4f.
    Here 4f = 4(20.0) = 80.0 cm < 90.0 cm, confirming the physical validity of two distinct conjugate positions. If D < 4f, no real image can form on the screen for any lens position.

Core1A physical construction

Transitioning from single-point lens calculations to the global quadratic solution space of conjugate foci on a fixed screen D, proving the existence of two distinct real positions and dual magnified/diminished states.

  1. 1. REPRESENT: Set up optical axis with object at origin and screen at x = D, placing lens at position u. Why valid: Enforces spatial constraint u + v = D with Cartesian signs u = -|u|, v = D - |u|.
  2. 2. MODEL: Substitute into thin lens formula 1/(D - u) + 1/u = 1/f to construct quadratic equation u^2 - Du + Df = 0. Why valid: Eliminates variable v to express lens position strictly in terms of constants D and f.
  3. 3. SYNTHESIZE: Evaluate quadratic discriminant Delta = D^2 - 4Df and extract roots u1, u2. Why valid: Delta > 0 guarantees two physical lens positions separated by d = sqrt(D^2 - 4Df).
  4. 4. JUSTIFY: Compute lateral magnifications m1 = v1/u1 and m2 = v2/u2, verifying m1 * m2 = 1. Why valid: Proves that one image is magnified (|m1| > 1) and the other is diminished (|m2| < 1).

An object and a screen are placed D = 90 cm apart. A convex lens of focal length f = 20 cm forms sharp images on the screen at two positions. Determine both lens positions and their magnifications.

  1. 1. System formulation: u^2 - Du + Df = 0 ==> u^2 - 90u + (90)(20) = u^2 - 90u + 1800 = 0.
  2. 2. Discriminant: Delta = (-90)^2 - 4(1)(1800) = 8100 - 7200 = 900 = 30^2 > 0.
  3. 3. Lens positions: u1 = (90 - 30)/2 = 30 cm (v1 = 60 cm); u2 = (90 + 30)/2 = 60 cm (v2 = 30 cm).
  4. 4. Magnifications: |m1| = 60/30 = 2.0 (magnified); |m2| = 30/60 = 0.5 (diminished). Note m1 * m2 = 1.0.
