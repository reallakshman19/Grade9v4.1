Fluid Mechanics — Buoyancy in Accelerating Frames

Anchor Item: PHYS-NSEJS-FLUID-01 | Band: D2 | Cognitive Demand: MODEL | Crux Move: M2

National Standard Examination in Junior Science (NSEJS) Reference Item

Problem Statement

A solid block of density ρs = 600 kg/m3 and mass m = 3.0 kg is tied to the bottom of a container filled with water (ρw = 1000 kg/m3) by a light inextensible cord. The entire container is placed inside an elevator accelerating vertically upwards with constant acceleration a = 2.0 m/s2. Taking g = 10.0 m/s2:

Determine the tension T in the cord during upward acceleration, and the initial upward acceleration arel of the block relative to the container immediately after the cord is severed.

Select Option:


Mathematical Physics Derivation & Proof

1. System Representation & Setup: In the upward accelerating elevator frame (a = 2.0 m/s2), the effective downward acceleration due to gravity is geff = g + a = 10.0 + 2.0 = 12.0 m/s2.

2. Physical Model & Formulation: Volume of the solid block: V = m / ρs = 3.0 / 600 = 0.0050 m3. Buoyant force in the accelerating frame: FB = ρw × V × geff = 1000 × 0.0050 × 12.0 = 60.0 N. Effective weight of the block: Weff = m × geff = 3.0 × 12.0 = 36.0 N.

3. Execution & Resolution: From vertical equilibrium prior to severance: FB = Weff + T ⇒ T = FB - Weff = 60.0 - 36.0 = 24.0 N. Immediately upon cutting the cord, the net upward force in the elevator frame is Fnet = FB - Weff = 24.0 N. The relative upward acceleration is arel = Fnet / m = 24.0 / 3.0 = 8.0 m/s2. Matching Option B.

4. Limiting Check: As a → -g (free fall), geff → 0, producing T = 0 N and arel = 0 m/s2.

Core 1A: Declarative Teaching Construction

Prerequisite

Effective Gravity & Accelerated Pressure Gradients

A fluid accelerating upwards at rate a requires a steeper hydrostatic pressure gradient ∂P/∂y = -ρ(g + a) to accelerate the liquid elements. Consequently, Archimedes' buoyant force scales directly with effective gravity: FB = ρfluid × V × (g + a).

Worked Example

Ascending Rocket Sensor Mooring Tether

A sensor package of mass m = 2.0 kg and density ρs = 500 kg/m3 is moored by a tether in brine (ρf = 1200 kg/m3) inside a rocket accelerating vertically upwards at a = 6.0 m/s2 with g = 10.0 m/s2.

1. Effective gravity: geff = g + a = 10.0 + 6.0 = 16.0 m/s2.

2. Volume: V = 2.0 / 500 = 0.0040 m3. Buoyant force: FB = 1200 × 0.0040 × 16.0 = 76.8 N. Effective weight: Weff = 2.0 × 16.0 = 32.0 N.

3. Tether tension: T = 76.8 - 32.0 = 44.8 N. Relative severance acceleration: arel = 44.8 / 2.0 = 22.4 m/s2 upward.

Independent Exit Check

Downward Accelerating Elevator Check

For m = 4.0 kg, ρs = 800 kg/m3 in water with a = 2.0 m/s2 downward: geff = 8.0 m/s2, V = 0.0050 m3, FB = 40.0 N, Weff = 32.0 N ⇒ T = 8.0 N, arel = 2.0 m/s2.

Stage 4 — Diagnose your first divergence

Common Divergence Traps:
  • Trap M1 (Static Buoyancy Fallacy): Using stationary g = 10.0 m/s² for buoyant force instead of g_eff = 14.0 m/s².
  • Trap M2 (Missing Pseudo-Force): Solving in the elevator frame without adding fictitious downward inertial force -m*a.
  • Trap M3 (Direct Mass Error): Dividing buoyant force by total fluid mass rather than canister mass m = 2.0 kg.
