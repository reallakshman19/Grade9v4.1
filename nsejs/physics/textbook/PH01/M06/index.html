1. Observe and establish a reference

A ball rolls up a ramp, momentarily stops, and rolls back; the same position may be visited at two distinct times.

Prerequisite bridge: PH01-M02; PH01-M03; PH01-M04; PH01-M05. Use a fixed reference axis and consistent SI units unless stated otherwise.

Learning objective: Derive, select and validate constant-acceleration laws, including root and reversal conditions.

2. Build and justify the scientific model

For a constant signed acceleration, the velocity graph is a straight line. Its slope gives v=u+at. The signed area of the rectangle of height u and triangle of height at gives Δx=ut+½at², even when part of the graph lies below the axis. The trapezoid expression uses the arithmetic mean of endpoint velocities only because the graph is linear. Multiplying v−u=at by v+u and substituting the area gives v²−u²=2aΔx, without dividing by a. Squaring loses velocity sign, and a position equation can yield two legitimate times when a cart passes a point on either side of a reversal. Treat roots as candidate times until the stage domain, direction and endpoint are checked. Find total distance by splitting at any zero of velocity, not by taking the absolute value of total displacement.

For constant acceleration: v=u+at; Δx=ut+½at²; Δx=(u+v)t/2; v²=u²+2aΔx. The last relation alone cannot determine a signed velocity. Use the full time domain and split actual path at a reversal.

Derivation and reasoning

  1. Read the straight v–t slope to obtain v=u+at. Constant acceleration means the slope does not vary.
  2. Compute signed rectangle plus triangle area, or trapezoid area. The integral of signed velocity is displacement.
  3. Multiply v−u=at by v+u and use Δx=(u+v)t/2. This eliminates elapsed time without assuming a nonzero acceleration.

3. Worked examples

FOUNDATION · worked example

Problem: A cart starts from rest with a=+2 m/s² for 4 s.

Reasoning: v=8 m/s and Δx=16 m.

Independent check: Average v=4 m/s times 4 s gives 16 m.

CONNECTED · worked example

Problem: A cart starts +6 m/s, accelerates −2 m/s² for 4 s.

Reasoning: v=−2 m/s; Δx=24−16=8 m. It travels 9 m to its turn at 3 s and 1 m back, D=10 m.

Independent check: Net displacement 8 m is not the full path.

ADVANCED · worked example

Problem: A cart starts +10 m/s, a=−2 m/s², and passes x=16 m from x0=0. Find times and velocity signs.

Reasoning: 10t−t²=16 gives t=2 or 8 s; v=+6 or −6 m/s respectively, if motion continues for both.

Independent check: Both times satisfy the position law, with different signed velocities.

4. Model limits and misconception repair

  • v² relation loses sign information; recover sign from time/domain.
  • Displacement is not distance when velocity reverses.
  • Variable acceleration needs a different model or piecewise intervals.
Wrong model: The positive square root of v² always gives the physical final velocity.

Diagnostic: A cart that has reversed can revisit the same position with negative velocity.

Repair: Use v=u+at and the valid time interval to recover sign; verify each root in the original equations.

5. Test your understanding

u=+4 m/s, a=−2 m/s² for 5 s. Find final velocity, displacement and distance.

Reveal textbook explanation

v=−6 m/s, Δx=−5 m, D=13 m. Turn at 2 s, x=4 m; final x=−5 m, giving 4+9 m.

Existing Core2 question preparation

One shared duration, two physical models

Two carts begin at the same speed u. One maintains u while the other decelerates uniformly to rest in time T. The first velocity area is uT; the second is uT/2. The area ratio is 1/2 without needing the numerical acceleration. The conclusion requires the same initial speed, same duration and uniform braking to rest; changing a condition may change the ratio.

R4.2 · Source-led advanced construction

Use a separate signed motion law in each region; carry position and velocity through a boundary unless an impact is specified. If entering with speed u>0 and acceleration −k, the same boundary is revisited after 2u/k, with velocity −u. Penetration is u²/(2k). For u=6 and k=2, turn after 3 s at depth 9 m, return after 6 s; the two traversals contribute 18 m path but zero displacement. For two particles moving along differently oriented axes, first use the valid motion law separately to obtain each endpoint coordinate along its own axis. Then translate both endpoints into one common plane as taught in M01; physical separation is a vector range, not subtraction of the two scalar distances unless the directions coincide.
