1. Observe and establish a reference

A vehicle's recorded position can pause, rise or fall while time keeps increasing; the slopes tell its signed motion.

Prerequisite bridge: PH01-M01 graph axes and scaled measurements; PH01-M03 distinction between distance and displacement; PH01-M04 acceleration and signed rates. Use a fixed reference axis and consistent SI units unless stated otherwise.

Learning objective: Model, translate and synthesize motion records using slopes, signed areas and cross-representation checks.

2. Build and justify the scientific model

To read a motion graph, first name both axes. The height of an x–t graph is position, not speed. A horizontal x–t segment means that position is temporarily unchanged, whereas an upward slope means positive signed velocity and a downward slope means negative signed velocity. To obtain a velocity–time graph from a piecewise linear position record, compute the slope for every valid interval and place that signed velocity on the corresponding time segment. Conversely, the signed area under a velocity–time curve reconstructs each position increment, so the endpoint is independently checkable. For distance, however, the magnitudes of areas are added. Crossing the horizontal position axis does not necessarily mean the vehicle has stopped; the slope at that crossing decides velocity. Changing the coordinate origin shifts the whole x–t graph vertically without changing any velocity–time segment. This is a core representation-invariance check. A finite position table alone does not determine instantaneous slopes or hidden reversals: explicitly state linear interpolation or another valid motion model.

Position–time slope gives velocity, velocity–time slope gives acceleration, and signed area under velocity–time gives displacement. Units independently distinguish the operations: m/s, m/s² and m. A finite table alone gives only interval averages unless interpolation is specified. When velocity changes linearly, use signed trapezoid or triangle areas between times. For distance, split any zero crossing and sum magnitudes; for a position sketch, match the tangent slopes to the given velocity. This graph-area construction prepares the motion equations in M06.

Derivation and reasoning

  1. Identify whether y-axis reports position or signed velocity. A height on x–t measures position, but a height on v–t measures velocity; the mathematical action depends on the axis.
  2. Use Δx/Δt to obtain interval velocity; preserve sign and duration. The slope of each straight position segment equals the constant velocity in that interval.
  3. Reconstruct total displacement from signed v×Δt areas and then independently calculate distance. Signed areas add to endpoint displacement, while actual travelled distance sums absolute interval lengths.

3. Worked examples

FOUNDATION · worked example

Problem: A cart's position rises from +1 m to +9 m over 4 s as a straight x–t line. Find velocity.

Reasoning: The signed slope is (9−1)/4=+2 m/s.

Independent check: Back substitution +1+2×4=+9 m.

CONNECTED · worked example

Problem: A piecewise-linear graph has x=3 at t=0, x=9 at t=3, and x=9 at t=5; reconstruct velocities.

Reasoning: First slope +2 m/s during 0–3; second slope 0 during 3–5. The second interval is stationary at position +9 m.

Independent check: Signed areas +6 and 0 recover Δx=+6 m.

ADVANCED · worked example

Problem: An object has velocity +4 m/s for 2 s, then −2 m/s for 5 s. Construct its x–t endpoint from x0=+3 m.

Reasoning: Displacement is 8−10=−2 m, final position x=+1 m. The x–t graph first increases, then decreases past its starting height.

Independent check: The signed area −2 m matches x_final−x_initial=−2; distance is 18 m.

4. Model limits and misconception repair

  • A graph's shape in paper coordinates is not the physical trajectory in space.
  • A position-axis crossing says nothing by itself about velocity direction.
  • A sharp velocity jump on an idealized step graph is not a real finite-acceleration process.
  • For smoothly changing velocity, use the actual area rather than constant-velocity rectangles.
  • Finite position samples alone do not specify the instantaneous velocity or exact path between samples.
Wrong model: A downward line on a velocity–time graph means the object is going down a hill.

Diagnostic: Compare the velocity graph and its axis labels to a position–time sketch of the same object.

Repair: Read the plotted quantity and its units before interpreting the slope or signed area.

5. Test your understanding

For v=+2 m/s during 4 s, then −3 m/s during 2 s, calculate net displacement and path distance.

Reveal textbook explanation

Net displacement +8−6=+2 m; path distance 8+6=14 m. Signed area of the second segment is negative, but travelled distance remains positive.

Existing Core2 question preparation

Translate and compare velocity histories

Acceleration is the slope of v–t; displacement is its signed area; distance is its absolute area. Acceleration–time area supplies change in velocity, not displacement. Equal areas under different graphs can coexist with different accelerations. If P is the positive area and N the negative-area magnitude, distance and displacement give independent equations P+N=D and P−N=Δx.

Source-driven worked anchor

A graph gives displacement +8 m and distance 18 m. Then P=13 m and N=5 m. If the negative region is a triangle ending at −5 m/s, the negative interval must last 2 s because ½×5×2=5. The two measurements constrain opposite-sign areas separately. Similar triangles connect the zero-crossing interval to the duration of a linearly falling segment.

R4.2 · Source-led advanced construction

A periodic or oscillating position graph has a displacement axis, peak amplitudes and a time between successive corresponding peaks. A shrinking envelope is different from a shrinking period; x=0 indicates the mean position, not necessarily rest. The energy explanation of damping belongs to the appropriate cross-topic lesson.

R4.3 · JUSTIFY construction · D3.7

For any integrable velocity history, signed area is displacement and absolute area is distance. If v≥0 everywhere, both coincide. Equal area does not constrain slope: constant 2 m/s over 4 s and a line from 0 to 4 m/s over 4 s both have area 8 m, but accelerations differ. A line from −2 to +6 over 4 s has the same signed area, yet a negative triangle of 1 m makes distance 10 m. State the condition whenever turning equal signed areas into equal distances.

R4.4 · EXPLAIN — causal chain · D3.2

A velocity change acquired early affects more of the subsequent position history than the same change acquired late. For example, two carts start at 2 m/s; one increases speed by 3 m/s early, then coasts, while the other acquires the same increase later. Their final speeds agree because their signed acceleration areas agree. The early cart carries its extra velocity for longer; its v–t area is larger, hence it has larger displacement. If the same change is a reduction, the ordering reverses. This is the missing causal bridge between a–t and x–t.

R4.4 · JUSTIFY — restricted theorem and converse · D4.7

Swapping two acceleration stages changes when velocity is acquired. For durations p,q and rates a,b, the endpoint velocity u+ap+bq is invariant. For the first ordering, x_X=up+ap²/2+(u+ap)q+bq²/2; reverse ordering yields x_Y=uq+bq²/2+(u+bq)p+ap²/2. Thus x_X−x_Y=(a−b)pq. This exact warrant says nothing by itself about total distance: |v| must be integrated separately when signs differ. A reversal-free nonnegative condition licenses distance=displacement; without it, construct opposite-direction equal-path examples.

NSEJS 2022 Q35 · REPRESENT transfer · D3.5 candidate

An a–t plot fixes velocity changes, not absolute velocity. Starting from v₀, carry Δv across each stage; a=0 preserves the carried velocity. The v–t slope is a, its signed area is Δx, and the x–t slope is v. Distance requires separate absolute areas across each zero of v. Worked anchor: x₀=0, v₀=−2, a=+2 for 2 s, then 0 for 1 s, then −1 for 2 s. Velocity vertices −2,2,2,0; signed displacement 0+2+2=4 m; distance 2+2+2=6 m. The initial reversal is at t=1 s, x=−1 m. Verify x_final=4 and the coast is moving.

Independent check: x_final=4 m from stage equations and signed areas; coast velocity remains +2.

NSEJS 2024 Q60 · JUSTIFY transfer · D3.7 candidate

Graph shapes cannot be compared by apparent steepness if axes differ. Convert time and velocity to common units; slope gives signed acceleration, signed area gives displacement, absolute area gives path. Equal areas need not mean equal average speeds unless durations agree. A vertical shift of a velocity graph changes signed area but leaves slopes unchanged. Worked anchor: A rises 0→8 m/s in 40 s; B is +4 m/s for 40 s. Both travel 160 m with average speed4, but accelerations 0.2 and0. Shift A downward by4: signed area0, path80 m, acceleration unchanged. Test each claim separately, using an actual counterexample rather than merely saying a converse is false.

Independent check: Slope Δv/Δt remains8/40=0.2 after shifting.
