1. Observe and establish a reference

A commuter can end near the starting point after a long journey: the speedometer and displacement tell different stories.

Prerequisite bridge: PH01-M01 reference frame and positive direction; PH01-M02 signed displacement. Use a fixed reference axis and consistent SI units unless stated otherwise.

Learning objective: Choose, represent and invert distance/displacement and speed/velocity distinctions in multi-leg motion.

2. Build and justify the scientific model

A description of motion begins with position, not the answer to a numerical formula. Mark the commuter's origin and east-positive coordinate. Each trip leg changes the position by a signed amount, but each contributes a positive path length. Average speed is the accumulated path length divided by elapsed time; average velocity is the single net position change divided by the same time. The two averages can differ even for a perfectly continuous trip, and average velocity becomes zero on a round trip. Reversing the coordinate direction reverses the velocity component without changing any physical speed. Build both numerical sums from the travel log before deciding which one the experiment measures. A graph of position versus time makes the change in direction visible, while a ledger of distances preserves how far the person actually travelled.

For elapsed time T, average speed = total path/T and signed average velocity = (x_f−x_i)/T. A time-weighted mean of stage velocities equals the interval average; an unweighted mean generally does not. A rest interval adds time but no distance.

Derivation and reasoning

  1. Mark every leg with its signed displacement and duration. Displacement is the difference between endpoint positions, and its sign depends on the chosen axis.
  2. Sum signed legs for Δx, and absolute leg lengths for path distance. A reversal cancels some signed displacement but never cancels distance already travelled.
  3. Divide the appropriate numerator by the same total elapsed time. The definition of each average requires the corresponding path or net position quantity.

3. Worked examples

FOUNDATION · worked example

Problem: A pupil walks 18 m east and 7 m west in 5 s. Compare distance and displacement.

Reasoning: Distance 18+7=25 m, signed displacement 18−7=+11 m, average speed 5 m/s, average velocity +2.2 m/s.

Independent check: 25≥11 and both averages carry m/s.

CONNECTED · worked example

Problem: A runner travels east 40 m in 8 s then west 40 m in 12 s. What does a motion tracker report?

Reasoning: Distance is 80 m and displacement is 0; average speed is 4 m/s and average velocity is zero.

Independent check: Returning to origin confirms Δx=0 despite movement.

ADVANCED · worked example

Problem: A cyclist's outward and return legs together total 54 m, with final position +6 m relative to origin. Recover east/west leg lengths.

Reasoning: Write E+W=54 and E−W=6. Solving gives E=30 m and W=24 m. This reconstructs route lengths, not merely an average.

Independent check: 30+24=54 and 30−24=6; both lengths are nonnegative.

4. Model limits and misconception repair

  • Interval averages do not determine detailed instantaneous velocity.
  • Different direction conventions change signed velocity, not path length.
  • A single scalar speed does not carry the direction needed for a meeting equation.
Wrong model: Average velocity equals average speed whenever elapsed time is the same.

Diagnostic: Ask for both averages on an equal-distance out-and-back walk.

Repair: Use net signed displacement as the numerator of average velocity, and total travelled distance for average speed.

5. Test your understanding

Travel +12 m in 4 s then −5 m in 1 s. Give both averages and direction.

Reveal textbook explanation

Total distance 17 m; net displacement +7 m; average speed 3.4 m/s; average velocity +1.4 m/s. The westward segment is negative in the signed sum but positive in the distance sum.

Existing Core2 question preparation

Compare different motion histories

Equal elapsed times and equal distances imply equal average speeds, not equal instantaneous speeds. A cart moving at 5 m/s for 6 s covers the same 30 m as one accelerating uniformly from rest to 10 m/s over 6 s. Their velocity areas agree although their slopes and terminal velocities differ.

R4.2 · Source-led advanced construction

When distance D and displacement Δ are measured for east and west legs, E=(D+Δ)/2 and W=(D−Δ)/2. Convert km/h by 5/18, m/min by 1/60, then time=leg length/speed. Example D=100 m, Δ=60 m, speeds 4 and 2 m/s: E=80, W=20, moving time=20+10=30 s. If elapsed=50 s, rest=20 s; a recorded 15 s rest is inconsistent.
