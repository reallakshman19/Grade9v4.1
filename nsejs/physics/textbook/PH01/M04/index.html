1. Observe and establish a reference

A trolley rolling west slows, stops and rolls east when a steady eastward acceleration acts.

Prerequisite bridge: PH01-M01; PH01-M03. Use a fixed reference axis and consistent SI units unless stated otherwise.

Learning objective: Infer signed acceleration, detect reversals and distinguish velocity change from speed change.

2. Build and justify the scientific model

Velocity specifies speed and direction; acceleration measures how velocity changes, not merely whether speed increases. On a fixed east-positive axis, an object with negative velocity can have positive acceleration: initially it slows, then it may reverse. Divide the signed change in velocity by elapsed time to obtain average acceleration. Only with a constant-acceleration model is this also the value throughout the interval. To determine speed change, compare the signs of instantaneous velocity and acceleration. If both have the same sign, the magnitude of velocity grows; if they have opposite signs, it shrinks. At zero velocity the sign rule needs care: the next interval determines the direction of motion. A velocity-time graph separates velocity sign (height), acceleration sign (slope) and a reversal (crossing zero).

For a constant acceleration interval, v(t)=u+at. A speed change depends on the magnitude |v|; at a zero crossing the motion changes direction if the signed velocity changes sign.

Derivation and reasoning

  1. Write initial and final velocities on a declared axis. The velocity change is a difference of signed components.
  2. Compute a_avg=(v−u)/Δt; use a constant slope only when justified. The quotient is an interval rate, not automatically an instantaneous rate.
  3. Check the sign of v and a on each interval, splitting at v=0. Speed is |v|, so its change depends on both signs.

3. Worked examples

FOUNDATION · worked example

Problem: A bike changes velocity from +2 to +8 m/s in 3 s.

Reasoning: Average acceleration is +2 m/s².

Independent check: Both velocities eastward; speed increases over the interval.

CONNECTED · worked example

Problem: A trolley changes uniformly from −6 to −2 m/s in 2 s.

Reasoning: a=+2 m/s², but speed falls from 6 to 2 m/s.

Independent check: Positive acceleration can slow a westbound object.

ADVANCED · worked example

Problem: A cart starts at −8 m/s with constant +2 m/s² for 6 s. Identify the reversal.

Reasoning: v=−8+2t, zero at 4 s, final +4 m/s. Speed falls for 4 s and then rises.

Independent check: The zero is inside the interval; a remains positive throughout.

4. Model limits and misconception repair

  • An interval average does not imply constant acceleration.
  • At a reversal v passes through zero; direction of acceleration is not itself direction of motion.
Wrong model: Positive acceleration always means speeding up.

Diagnostic: A westbound cart has a positive acceleration while its velocity is negative.

Repair: Use the sign of the velocity together with acceleration; split the motion at zero velocity.

5. Test your understanding

A cart changes uniformly from +5 to −1 m/s in 3 s. Find acceleration and time of reversal.

Reveal textbook explanation

a=−2 m/s²; reversal at 2.5 s. v=5−2t=0 at t=2.5 s, within the interval.
