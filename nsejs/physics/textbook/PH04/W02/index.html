Spring contact, stored energy and motion phases

A1 · Observe and ask

A pinball launcher propelled by a compressed spring shoots forward rapidly while touching the spring, but once the spring expands to its natural length, the ball detaches and coasts freely across the smooth table. If it then hits a felt carpet strip, friction begins acting only on that strip.

Learning target

Model piecewise motion phases across spring contact and subsequent uncoupled resistive zones

A3 · Construct the model

An unattached spring exerts force only when compressed (x > 0). While in contact, stored elastic potential energy U_s = 0.5*k*x^2 converts into kinetic energy. Once the spring reaches natural length (x = 0), contact ends and the spring can no longer do work on the detached body. Subsequent motion must be modeled using a fresh work-energy ledger governed only by forces present in that subsequent region.

A4 · Derive and check the rule

Elastic potential energy: U_s = 0.5 * k * x^2. Detachment boundary condition: at x = 0, normal contact force F_contact = 0. Free coasting or resistive phase: Delta K = W_resistive = -f * d. Total piecewise energy conservation: 0.5 * k * x_0^2 + W_external = 0.5 * m * v_final^2. Dimensions: [U_s] = M L^2 T^-2. Validity: ideal linear Hookean springs obeying F = -k*x without mass or internal damping.

Check the frame, sign convention, unit and applicability before accepting a numerical result. For a new model the original scientific warrant still requires independent review.

A5 · Read the physical representation

A6 · Three progressive worked examples

FOUNDATION

Question: A 1 kg cart at rest is released by an unattached spring (k = 100 N/m, x = 0.20 m) on a smooth track. Find its release speed.

Reasoned solution: U_s = 0.5 * 100 * (0.2)^2 = 2 J. K = 2 J ==> v = sqrt(2 * 2 / 1) = 2.0 m s^-1.

CONNECTED

Question: The same cart with 2 J spring energy reaches a rough strip of length 1 m with friction force 1.5 N. Find its final speed.

Reasoned solution: W_fric = -1.5 * 1 = -1.5 J. Final K_f = 2 - 1.5 = 0.5 J ==> v_f = sqrt(2 * 0.5 / 1) = 1.0 m s^-1.

ADVANCED

Question: A vertical spring launcher (k = 400 N/m, x = 0.10 m) launches a 0.2 kg ball straight up. Find the maximum height reached above release point.

Reasoned solution: Stored energy U_s = 0.5 * 400 * (0.01) = 2 J. Gravity work W_grav = -m * g * h = -0.2 * 10 * h = -2 * h. At max height K = 0 ==> 2 - 2*h = 0 ==> h = 1.0 m.

A7 · Worked anchor and independent check

Problem: A 1 kg cart starts at rest, touching a horizontal spring compressed by 0.20 m with stiffness 100 N/m. The cart is not attached to the spring and the track is smooth throughout. What is its speed at natural length, and what happens after release?

Solution and check: Stored spring energy: U_s = 0.5 * 100 * (0.20)^2 = 2 J. At natural length, U_s = 0, so kinetic energy K = 2 J. Release speed: v = sqrt(2 * 2 / 1) = 2 m/s. After release, the unattached spring exerts no force, so the cart continues at constant speed 2 m/s.

A8 · Diagnose a tempting wrong idea

Tempting claim: An unattached spring continues to pull or accelerate the cart after passing its natural length.

Why it fails: A contact spring without physical attachment can only push under compression; it cannot exert tension to pull a detached mass.

Repair: Always divide the path into two distinct phases: contact phase (x > 0) and uncoupled phase (x <= 0).

A9 · Counterexample, limit and synthesis

Limiting case verification

If the spring is rigidly fastened to the cart, it executes simple harmonic oscillation rather than detaching at natural length.

Unassisted synthesis challenge

Question: A 0.5 kg cart released by an unattached spring with 4 J stored energy hits a rough strip with 2 N friction. How far does it slide before stopping?

Model route: Distance d = U_s / f = 4 / 2 = 2.0 metres.
