Distributed mass and continuous center-of-mass work

A1 · Observe and ask

Pulling a heavy hanging chain onto a table feels much harder at the very beginning when most of the chain is dangling below the edge, but becomes progressively easier as more links rest on the tabletop.

Learning target

Calculate work done against gravity for variable hanging continuous mass chains and ropes

A3 · Construct the model

For a continuous uniform body of linear mass density lambda = M/L, the portion hanging vertically of length x has mass m(x) = lambda*x. Because the hanging mass is uniformly distributed, its center of mass is located at depth x/2 below the table. Taking the tabletop as potential energy zero, the potential energy of the hanging portion is U(x) = -m(x)*g*(x/2) = -0.5*lambda*g*x^2. When the hanging length changes from x_1 to x_2, the work required against gravity is W = U(x_2) - U(x_1) = 0.5*lambda*g*(x_1^2 - x_2^2). Work depends quadratically on hanging length.

A4 · Derive and check the rule

Continuous potential energy: U(x) = -0.5 * lambda * g * x^2, where lambda = M / L. Work against gravity: W_lift = U(x_final) - U(x_initial) = 0.5 * lambda * g * (x_initial^2 - x_final^2). Center of mass depth: y_cm = x / 2. Dimensions: [U] = M L^2 T^-2. Validity: uniform flexible chain/rope, quasi-static lifting without kinetic energy gain or friction.

Check the frame, sign convention, unit and applicability before accepting a numerical result. For a new model the original scientific warrant still requires independent review.

A6 · Three progressive worked examples

FOUNDATION

Question: A uniform 2 m chain of 1 kg (lambda = 0.5 kg/m) has 1 m hanging. Find the work to lift it completely onto the table.

Reasoned solution: Initial potential energy U_i = -0.5 * 0.5 * 10 * (1^2) = -2.5 J. Work against gravity W = 0 - (-2.5) = +2.5 J.

CONNECTED

Question: A uniform 4 m chain of mass 2 kg (lambda = 0.5 kg/m) has 2 m hanging (U_i = -10 J). Find the work to lift it fully onto the table.

Reasoned solution: Hanging mass = 1 kg at depth 1 m ==> U_i = -1 * 10 * 1 = -10 J. Final U_f = 0 J. Work W = 0 - (-10) = +10 J.

ADVANCED

Question: A uniform 6 m rope of mass 3 kg (lambda = 0.5 kg/m) released from rest with 1 m hanging slips freely off a smooth table. Find its speed when it completely leaves the table.

Reasoned solution: U_i = -0.5 * 0.5 * 10 * 1^2 = -2.5 J. U_f = -0.5 * 0.5 * 10 * 6^2 = -90 J. Delta K = -Delta U = 90 - 2.5 = 87.5 J ==> v = sqrt(2 * 87.5 / 3) = sqrt(58.33) ≈ 7.64 m s^-1.

A7 · Worked anchor and independent check

Problem: A uniform 4 m chain has mass 2 kg. Half of its length, 2 m, hangs vertically over a smooth table edge while the other half rests on the table. A helper pulls it very slowly until the whole chain lies on the tabletop. The table is smooth and g = 10 m/s^2. How much work must the helper do against gravity?

Solution and check: Linear mass density: lambda = 2 kg / 4 m = 0.5 kg/m. The hanging 2 m piece has mass m_h = 0.5 * 2 = 1 kg. Its center of mass is halfway down, at depth h_cm = 2 / 2 = 1 m below the table. Initial potential energy relative to table zero: U_i = -m_h * g * h_cm = -(1)(10)(1) = -10 J. At the end, the entire chain is on the table, so U_f = 0 J. Work done by helper: W_lift = U_f - U_i = 0 - (-10) = +10 J.

A8 · Diagnose a tempting wrong idea

Tempting claim: To lift the hanging chain, multiply the total mass 2 kg by the hanging length 2 m to get work 40 J.

Why it fails: Only the hanging 1 kg portion changes height, and its center of mass rises by only 1 m, not the full 2 m.

Repair: Always use the hanging mass lambda*x and its center of mass depth x/2: U = -0.5*lambda*g*x^2.

A9 · Counterexample, limit and synthesis

Limiting case verification

If all 4 m of the chain hang vertically, initial center of mass depth is 2 m, so lifting it completely requires 2 * 10 * 2 = 40 J.

Unassisted synthesis challenge

Question: What fraction of the total work to lift a completely hanging chain is done in pulling up the first half?

Model route: W_first_half / W_total = (L^2 - (L/2)^2) / L^2 = (1 - 1/4) = 3/4 = 75%.
