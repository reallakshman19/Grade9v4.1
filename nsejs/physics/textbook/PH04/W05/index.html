Work and energy graphical operations: area versus slope

A1 · Observe and ask

In a laboratory notebook, two graphs look visually identical: both show a straight line rising from the origin to a peak. But on one graph the area represents work in joules, while on the other graph the slope represents velocity in m/s. Performing the wrong operation produces dimensionally meaningless numbers.

Learning target

Select and execute correct calculus operations (area vs slope) based on graph coordinate axes units

A3 · Construct the model

The physical meaning of graph features is dictated strictly by dimensional analysis of the coordinate axes: 1. Area = Integral of y dx has dimensions [y]*[x]. For a force-position graph [F_x vs x], the area has units N*m = J, representing work done: W = Integral F_x dx. 2. Slope = dy/dx has dimensions [y]/[x]. For a potential energy-position graph [U vs x], the slope has units J/m = N. Because conservative force opposes the potential gradient, F_x = -dU/dx. 3. For a displacement-time graph [s vs t], the slope has units m/s, representing velocity: v = ds/dt.

A4 · Derive and check the rule

Force-position area: W = Integral_{x_1}^{x_2} F_x(x) dx. Conservative force-potential slope: F_x = -dU/dx. Displacement-time slope: v = ds/dt. Dimensional consistency criteria: [Area] = [y] * [x], [Slope] = [y] / [x]. Dimensions: [W] = M L^2 T^-2, [F] = M L T^-2, [v] = L T^-1. Validity: piecewise continuous functions along a single spatial coordinate axis in an inertial reference frame.

Check the frame, sign convention, unit and applicability before accepting a numerical result. For a new model the original scientific warrant still requires independent review.

A5 · Read the physical representation

A6 · Three progressive worked examples

FOUNDATION

Question: Force rises from 0 to 6 N over 3 m on an F_x vs x graph. Find the work done.

Reasoned solution: Area of triangle W = 0.5 * 3 * 6 = 9.0 J.

CONNECTED

Question: Potential energy drops from 12 J to 4 J between x = 1 m and x = 3 m. Find the conservative force component.

Reasoned solution: Slope dU/dx = (4 - 12) / (3 - 1) = -4 J m^-1 ==> F_x = -dU/dx = -(-4) = +4.0 N.

ADVANCED

Question: A particle has potential energy U(x) = 4*x^2 - 16*x J. Find equilibrium position and force at x = 1 m.

Reasoned solution: F_x = -dU/dx = -(8*x - 16) = 16 - 8*x. Equilibrium at F_x = 0 ==> x = 2 m. At x = 1 m: F_x = 16 - 8 = +8.0 N.

A7 · Worked anchor and independent check

Problem: A student inspects three notebook graphs: Graph A is F_x vs x (horizontal line at +3 N from 0 to 2 m); Graph B is U vs x (line from (0, 9 J) to (2 m, 3 J)); Graph C is s vs t (line from (0, 0) to (3 s, 6 m)). Determine work from Graph A, force from Graph B, and velocity from Graph C.

Solution and check: Graph A (F_x vs x): Area represents work: W = (3 N) * (2 m) = +6 J. Graph B (U vs x): Slope is dU/dx = (3 - 9)/(2 - 0) = -3 J/m. Conservative force is F_x = -dU/dx = -(-3) = +3 N. Graph C (s vs t): Slope is ds/dt = (6 - 0)/(3 - 0) = 2 m/s, which is velocity.

A8 · Diagnose a tempting wrong idea

Tempting claim: Area under any physics graph always represents work, and slope always represents acceleration.

Why it fails: Area under an s-t graph has units metre-seconds, which has no direct physical meaning. Area under U-x has units Joule-metres, not work.

Repair: Multiply axis units to check area: [N]*[m] = J (work). Divide axis units to check slope: [J]/[m] = N (force).

A9 · Counterexample, limit and synthesis

Limiting case verification

At a point where U(x) reaches a minimum or maximum, the slope dU/dx = 0, so the net conservative force is exactly zero (equilibrium point).

Unassisted synthesis challenge

Question: If the force-position curve is F_x = -k*x, what is the work done from x = 0 to x = x_0?

Model route: Area W = Integral_0^{x_0} (-k*x) dx = -0.5 * k * x_0^2.
