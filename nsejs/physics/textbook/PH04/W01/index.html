A1 · Observe and ask

A moving shipping crate pulled by a motor across a concrete floor speeds up if the motor pull exceeds friction, but slows down if the motor is cut while friction continues to act. Although both the motor and friction act simultaneously, their work contributes with opposite signs.

Learning target

Account for work contributions of multiple collinear and perpendicular forces to determine net work and velocity changes

A3 · Construct the model

Work done by a force on a moving body depends on the projection of force along displacement: W = F * d * cos(theta). The work-energy theorem states that the change in kinetic energy equals the algebraic sum of work done by all individual forces acting on the body: W_net = sum(W_i) = Delta K. Forces perpendicular to motion do zero work.

A4 · Derive and check the rule

Work-energy theorem: W_net = sum(W_i) = K_f - K_i = 0.5 * m * (v_f^2 - v_i^2). Signed work formula: W_i = F_i * d * cos(theta_i). Normal forces and perpendicular components: W_perp = 0. Dimensions: [W] = M L^2 T^-2 (J). Validity: point particles or rigid bodies under translational motion in an inertial reference frame.

Check the frame, sign convention, unit and applicability before accepting a numerical result. For a new model the original scientific warrant still requires independent review.

A5 · Read the physical representation

A6 · Three progressive worked examples

FOUNDATION

Question: A horizontal 10 N pull moves a 2 kg box 3 m across a smooth floor from rest. Find its final kinetic energy.

Reasoned solution: W = 10 * 3 = 30 J. K_f = K_i + W = 0 + 30 = 30 J ==> v_f = sqrt(2 * 30 / 2) ≈ 5.48 m s^-1.

CONNECTED

Question: A 2 kg box already moving right at 1 m s^-1 is pulled right by 10 N while 4 N friction opposes it over 3 m.

Reasoned solution: W_pull = +10 * 3 = +30 J. W_fric = -4 * 3 = -12 J. W_net = +18 J. K_i = 0.5 * 2 * 1^2 = 1 J. K_f = 1 + 18 = 19 J ==> v_f = sqrt(19) ≈ 4.36 m s^-1.

ADVANCED

Question: A 2 kg block slides down a 30° incline of length 4 m with kinetic friction coefficient mu = 0.2. Find its final speed from rest.

Reasoned solution: W_grav = m * g * (L * sin 30°) = 2 * 10 * 2 = 40 J. Normal N = m * g * cos 30° = 10 * sqrt(3) N. W_fric = -mu * N * L = -0.2 * 17.32 * 4 = -13.86 J. W_net = 40 - 13.86 = 26.14 J ==> v_f = sqrt(26.14) ≈ 5.11 m s^-1.

A7 · Worked anchor and independent check

Problem: A 2 kg box is already moving right at 1 m/s. A child pulls it horizontally to the right with a constant 10 N force, while friction is 4 N to the left. It travels 3 m. What is the net work done on the box, and what is its final speed?

Solution and check: W_pull = +10 * 3 = +30 J. W_fric = -4 * 3 = -12 J. Normal force and gravity act perpendicular to displacement: W_N = 0, W_grav = 0. Net work: W_net = 30 - 12 = +18 J. Initial kinetic energy: K_i = 0.5 * 2 * 1^2 = 1 J. Final kinetic energy: K_f = 1 + 18 = 19 J. Final speed: v_f = sqrt(2 * 19 / 2) = sqrt(19) ≈ 4.36 m/s.

A8 · Diagnose a tempting wrong idea

Tempting claim: Friction always does the only work that determines the change in kinetic energy.

Why it fails: Equating the work of a single force like friction to the total change in kinetic energy ignores active applied forces like motors or pulls.

Repair: Always compute the algebraic sum of all force work before applying Delta K: W_net = sum(W_i).

A9 · Counterexample, limit and synthesis

Limiting case verification

If motor pull equals friction (10 N = 10 N), W_net = 0, Delta K = 0, speed remains strictly constant.

Unassisted synthesis challenge

Question: A motorized cart moves horizontally 5 m under 20 N forward pull, 5 N friction, and climbs a 1 m vertical step. What is its net work?

Model route: W_net = 20*5 - 5*5 - m*g*1 = 100 - 25 - 10*m joules.
