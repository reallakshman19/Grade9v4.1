A1 · Observe and ask

Comet Halley orbits the Sun in a highly elongated elliptical path with an orbital period of about 76 years. When it approaches perihelion (closest approach to the Sun), it whips around at an astonishing speed of 54 km/s. When it reaches aphelion (farthest point, beyond Neptune), it slows down to a sluggish crawl of less than 1 km/s.

Learning target

Apply Kepler's 3rd law T^2 proportional to a^3 and 2nd law conservation of areal velocity dA/dt = L/(2m) to determine orbital periods, semimajor axes, and perihelion/aphelion speed ratios.

A3 · Construct the model

Kepler's Three Laws: (1) Law of Orbits: planets orbit in ellipses with the Sun at one focus. (2) Law of Areas: the position vector from the Sun to a planet sweeps out equal areas in equal intervals of time (dA/dt = const). This is a direct consequence of zero torque under central gravitational force: tau = r x F_g = 0 ==> L = r x p = const ==> dA/dt = L / (2m). (3) Law of Periods: the square of orbital period T is proportional to the cube of semimajor axis a: T^2 = (4*pi^2 / GM) * a^3.

A4 · Derive and check the rule

Kepler's Third Law: T^2 = (4 * pi^2 / G*M) * a^3. Areal velocity: dA/dt = L / (2m) = (1/2) * r * v_perp = const. At perihelion and aphelion, velocity is perpendicular to position vector: r_p * v_p = r_a * v_a. Dimensions: [T^2 / a^3] = L^-3 T^2. Validity: two-body bound Keplerian system where m_planet << M_sun.

Check the frame, sign convention, unit and applicability before accepting a numerical result. For a new model the original scientific warrant still requires independent review.

A5 · Read the physical representation

A6 · Three progressive worked examples

FOUNDATION

Question: A planet has an orbital radius 4 times that of Earth. Find its orbital period in Earth years.

Reasoned solution: T^2 proportional to a^3 ==> T/T_E = (a/a_E)^(3/2) = 4^(3/2) = (sqrt(4))^3 = 2^3 = 8 Earth years.

CONNECTED

Question: A planet has perihelion distance r_p = 2 AU and aphelion distance r_a = 6 AU. Find its semimajor axis and orbital period.

Reasoned solution: Semimajor axis a = (r_p + r_a) / 2 = (2 + 6) / 2 = 4 AU. Period T = a^(3/2) = 4^(3/2) = 8 years.

ADVANCED

Question: For the same planet with r_p = 2 AU and r_a = 6 AU, find the ratio of its speed at perihelion to its speed at aphelion.

Reasoned solution: By conservation of angular momentum at apsides: r_p * v_p = r_a * v_a ==> v_p / v_a = r_a / r_p = 6 / 2 = 3 : 1.

A7 · Worked anchor and independent check

Problem: Two satellites S1 and S2 revolve around Earth in coplanar circular orbits of radii r1 and r2 = 4*r1. Determine: (1) the ratio of their orbital periods T1/T2, (2) the ratio of their orbital speeds v1/v2.

Solution and check: (1) By Kepler's 3rd Law: T1 / T2 = (r1 / r2)^(3/2) = (1/4)^(3/2) = 1/8. (2) For circular orbits, v = sqrt(GM/r) ==> v1 / v2 = sqrt(r2 / r1) = sqrt(4) = 2. Notice consistency: T = 2*pi*r / v ==> T1/T2 = (r1/r2) * (v2/v1) = (1/4) * (1/2) = 1/8.

A8 · Diagnose a tempting wrong idea

Tempting claim: Planets travel at constant linear speed along their elliptical orbits.

Why it fails: Linear speed varies continuously. Speed is maximum at perihelion and minimum at aphelion. What remains constant is areal velocity (dA/dt), not linear speed (ds/dt).

Repair: Remember conservation of angular momentum: L = m * r * v_perp = const. As radial distance r decreases, perpendicular speed v_perp must increase.

A9 · Counterexample, limit and synthesis

Changed condition

Does Kepler's Third Law apply to a circular orbit?

Yes. A circle is an ellipse with eccentricity e = 0 and semimajor axis a = r. The relation T^2 = (4*pi^2 / GM)*r^3 holds identically.

Independent exit check

If the distance between Earth and the Sun were suddenly halved, how many days would there be in an Earth year?

Reveal the reasoned exit answer

New period T' = T0 * (a'/a0)^(3/2) = 365.25 * (1/2)^(3/2) = 365.25 / sqrt(8) ≈ 365.25 / 2.828 ≈ 129.1 days.
