Gravitational potential and escape velocity

A1 · Observe and ask

Throw a ball upward at 10 m/s and it climbs 5 meters before falling back. Launch a rocket at 11.2 km/s from Earth's surface, and it will never return—even with its engines turned off completely. At this speed, its kinetic energy overcomes the entire gravitational binding energy of Earth.

Learning target

Derive gravitational potential V(r) = -GM/r with zero-reference at infinity, prove escape velocity v_e = sqrt(2GM/R), and evaluate energy conservation for vertical and escape trajectories.

A3 · Construct the model

Gravitational potential energy U(r) is defined as the work done by an external agent in bringing a test mass m from infinity to position r without acceleration: U(r) = -G*M*m / r. For an object of mass m launched from surface radius R with velocity v, its total energy is E = (1/2)*m*v^2 - G*M*m/R. To escape to infinity where potential energy reaches its maximum value (0 J), the object must have E >= 0. Setting E = 0 yields minimum escape speed: (1/2)*m*v_e^2 = G*M*m/R ==> v_e = sqrt(2*G*M / R) = sqrt(2 * g0 * R) = sqrt(2) * v_orbital.

A4 · Derive and check the rule

Escape velocity: v_e = sqrt(2 * G * M / R) = sqrt(2 * g0 * R). On Earth: v_e ≈ sqrt(2 * 9.8 * 6.4 * 10^6) ≈ 11.2 km s^-1. Dimensions: [v_e] = L T^-1. Validity: non-rotating spherical body, neglecting atmospheric friction. Directional invariance: because energy E is a scalar, v_e is identical for ANY launch direction that clears the surface.

Check the frame, sign convention, unit and applicability before accepting a numerical result. For a new model the original scientific warrant still requires independent review.

A5 · Read the physical representation

A6 · Three progressive worked examples

FOUNDATION

Question: Calculate the ratio of escape speed v_e from the surface of Earth to circular orbital speed v_o near Earth's surface.

Reasoned solution: v_e / v_o = sqrt(2*GM/R) / sqrt(GM/R) = sqrt(2) ≈ 1.414.

CONNECTED

Question: A projectile is launched vertically from Earth's surface with speed v = v_e / 2. To what maximum altitude h above the surface does it rise?

Reasoned solution: Conservation of energy: (1/2)*m*(v_e/2)^2 - GMm/R = 0 - GMm/(R+h). Since v_e^2 = 2GM/R: (1/2)*m*(2GM/4R) - GMm/R = -GMm/(R+h) ==> (1/4 - 1)/R = -1/(R+h) ==> -3/(4R) = -1/(R+h) ==> R+h = 4R/3 ==> h = R/3.

ADVANCED

Question: A body is projected with speed v = 2 * v_e. Find its residual speed v_inf when it escapes to infinity in deep space.

Reasoned solution: Conservation of energy: (1/2)*m*v^2 - GMm/R = (1/2)*m*v_inf^2 + 0. Since GMm/R = (1/2)*m*v_e^2: (1/2)*m*(4*v_e^2) - (1/2)*m*v_e^2 = (1/2)*m*v_inf^2 ==> v_inf^2 = 3 * v_e^2 ==> v_inf = sqrt(3) * v_e ≈ 19.4 km/s.

A7 · Worked anchor and independent check

Problem: Planet Z has mass 4 times that of Earth and radius 9 times that of Earth. If escape speed on Earth is 11.2 km/s, what is the escape speed from Planet Z?

Solution and check: Escape speed formula v_e = sqrt(2*G*M / R). Ratio v_Z / v_E = sqrt((M_Z / M_E) / (R_Z / R_E)) = sqrt(4 / 9) = 2 / 3. Therefore v_Z = (2/3) * 11.2 km/s ≈ 7.47 km/s.

A8 · Diagnose a tempting wrong idea

Tempting claim: Escape velocity must be directed straight vertically upward to escape the planet.

Why it fails: Energy is a scalar quantity. Since total energy E = 0.5*m*v^2 - GMm/R depends on speed squared and not direction, any launch direction with v >= v_e will escape, provided the trajectory does not collide with the surface of the planet.

Repair: Escape velocity is properly termed 'escape speed'—it is a scalar threshold. Launching at 45° to the horizontal with speed v_e escapes along a parabolic trajectory.

A9 · Counterexample, limit and synthesis

Changed condition

Can a rocket escape Earth with a speed of only 10 km/h?

Yes, if it has continuous thrust! The escape speed v_e = 11.2 km/s is the ballistic speed for an unpowered projectile. A continuous engine can lift an object to infinity at any arbitrarily slow speed.

Independent exit check

What is the escape velocity from the surface of a black hole whose mass M is compressed within radius R_s = 2*G*M / c^2 (Schwarzschild radius)?

Reveal the reasoned exit answer

Setting v_e = c in the escape equation: c = sqrt(2*G*M / R_s) ==> R_s = 2*G*M / c^2. At this critical radius, escape speed equals the speed of light. Inside R_s, even light cannot escape.
