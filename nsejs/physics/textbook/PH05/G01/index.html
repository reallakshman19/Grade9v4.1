Universal gravitation and inverse-square law

A1 · Observe and ask

The Earth pulls a 1 kg stone downward with a force of about 9.8 N. By Newton's third law, the stone simultaneously pulls the entire Earth upward with an identical force of 9.8 N. Because the Earth's mass is immense, its resulting acceleration is unmeasurably tiny, yet the interaction forces are strictly equal and opposite.

Learning target

Calculate mutual gravitational attraction between two point or spherical masses, apply vector superposition, and verify Newton's 3rd Law pair symmetry for disparate masses.

A3 · Construct the model

Every particle in the universe attracts every other particle along the line joining their centers. By Newton's shell theorem, a spherically symmetric body of mass M acts gravitationally on any external object as if its entire mass were concentrated at its geometric center. Doubling the separation distance between centers reduces the mutual force to one-fourth of its initial magnitude.

A4 · Derive and check the rule

Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation: F = G * (m1 * m2) / r^2, where G = 6.674 * 10^-11 N m^2 kg^-2 is Cavendish's universal gravitational constant. The force vector is purely attractive and directed along the radial line joining the two centers. Dimensions: [F] = M L T^-2. Validity requires r measured between geometric centers of non-overlapping spherically symmetric masses or point particles in an inertial reference frame.

Check the frame, sign convention, unit and applicability before accepting a numerical result. For a new model the original scientific warrant still requires independent review.

A5 · Read the physical representation

A6 · Three progressive worked examples

FOUNDATION

Question: Two identical 10 kg spheres are placed with their centers 2 m apart in deep space.

Reasoned solution: F = G * (10 * 10) / (2^2) = G * 100 / 4 = 25 * G = 25 * 6.674 * 10^-11 ≈ 1.67 * 10^-9 N attractive.

CONNECTED

Question: The distance between two interacting spheres is tripled while one mass is doubled.

Reasoned solution: New force F' = G * (2 * m1 * m2) / (3r)^2 = (2/9) * (G * m1 * m2 / r^2) = (2/9) * F.

ADVANCED

Question: Three identical masses m are fixed at the vertices of an equilateral triangle of side L. Find the magnitude of the net gravitational force on any one mass.

Reasoned solution: Each neighbor exerts force F0 = G * m^2 / L^2 at an angle of 60°. The vector resultant is F_net = sqrt(F0^2 + F0^2 + 2 * F0 * F0 * cos 60°) = sqrt(3) * G * m^2 / L^2 directed toward the centroid.

A7 · Worked anchor and independent check

Problem: A 100 kg satellite and a 400 kg space station are separated by 10 m in an isolated orbit. Compare the gravitational force exerted by the station on the satellite with that exerted by the satellite on the station, and compute the satellite's initial acceleration toward the station.

Solution and check: By Newton's third law, F_satellite_on_station = F_station_on_satellite = G * (100 * 400) / (10^2) = 400 * G ≈ 2.67 * 10^-8 N. The satellite's acceleration is a_sat = F / m_sat = 2.67 * 10^-8 / 100 = 2.67 * 10^-10 m s^-2 toward the station.

A8 · Diagnose a tempting wrong idea

Tempting claim: The heavier body exerts a larger gravitational force on the lighter body than the lighter body exerts on the heavier one.

Why it fails: This confuses force with acceleration. The interaction force F = G * m1 * m2 / r^2 is symmetrical under exchange of m1 and m2; the lighter body accelerates more because a = F / m is larger for smaller m.

Repair: Verify Newton's third law pair: F12 = -F21 always. The mass asymmetry appears solely in the kinematic accelerations a1 = F/m1 and a2 = F/m2.

A9 · Counterexample, limit and synthesis

Changed condition

If the separation r approaches zero, does gravitational force approach infinity?

No for real extended bodies. The point-mass equation applies only outside the surfaces (r >= R1 + R2). When spheres touch, r cannot be smaller than R1 + R2.

Independent exit check

Two bodies of mass m and 4m are released from rest in deep space at distance d. Where do they collide relative to their initial positions?

Reveal the reasoned exit answer

Because internal gravitational forces create no net external impulse, the system's center of mass remains stationary. The center of mass is at x_cm = (4m * d) / (m + 4m) = 0.8d from mass m. They collide at this exact center of mass point.
