Circular satellite dynamics and orbital energy

A1 · Observe and ask

When a satellite in low Earth orbit encounters residual atmospheric friction, it loses mechanical energy to heat. Paradoxically, as friction slows it down over months, the satellite spirals inward and its orbital speed actually INCREASES. This surprising phenomenon is known as the satellite paradox.

Learning target

Derive circular orbital velocity v_o = sqrt(GM/r) and total mechanical energy E = -K = (1/2)U, and explain the satellite paradox where atmospheric drag causes orbital speed to increase.

A3 · Construct the model

For a stable circular orbit of radius r, gravitational attraction provides the necessary centripetal force: G*M*m/r^2 = m*v^2/r ==> v_o = sqrt(GM/r). Kinetic energy is K = (1/2)*m*v^2 = G*M*m / (2r). Potential energy is U = -G*M*m / r. Total mechanical energy is E = K + U = G*M*m/(2r) - G*M*m/r = -G*M*m / (2r). Notice that E = -K = (1/2)*U. When drag removes total energy E (making E more negative), r must decrease. But because v_o = sqrt(GM/r), a smaller radius r forces orbital speed v_o to rise!

A4 · Derive and check the rule

Orbital velocity: v_o = sqrt(G*M / r). Orbital period: T = 2*pi*r / v_o = 2*pi*sqrt(r^3 / G*M). Energy virial relation: K = -E = -U/2 = G*M*m / (2r). Dimensions: [E] = M L^2 T^-2. Validity: circular bound orbits under inverse-square central gravity.

Check the frame, sign convention, unit and applicability before accepting a numerical result. For a new model the original scientific warrant still requires independent review.

A6 · Three progressive worked examples

FOUNDATION

Question: A 500 kg satellite orbits Earth at radius r = 2 R_E. Write expressions for its kinetic energy K, potential energy U, and total energy E in terms of G, M_E, and R_E.

Reasoned solution: K = G*M_E*(500) / (4 R_E); U = -G*M_E*(500) / (2 R_E); E = K + U = -G*M_E*(500) / (4 R_E).

CONNECTED

Question: A satellite is moved from circular orbit of radius r1 to a higher circular orbit of radius r2 = 2*r1. What happens to its kinetic energy and total mechanical energy?

Reasoned solution: Kinetic energy K2 = K1 / 2 (decreases by half). Total energy E2 = E1 / 2 (since E is negative, e.g. -100 J to -50 J, total energy increases by +50 J).

ADVANCED

Question: How much work must rocket thrusters perform to transfer a satellite of mass m from circular orbit r1 to higher circular orbit r2?

Reasoned solution: Work done W = Delta_E = E2 - E1 = [-G*M*m / (2*r2)] - [-G*M*m / (2*r1)] = (G*M*m / 2) * (1/r1 - 1/r2) > 0.

A7 · Worked anchor and independent check

Problem: A satellite of mass m revolves in a circular orbit of radius r around Earth. What additional kinetic energy must be supplied to the satellite to shift it into a higher circular orbit of radius 3r?

Solution and check: Initial energy E1 = -GMm / (2r). Final energy E2 = -GMm / (2 * 3r) = -GMm / (6r). Required energy Delta_E = E2 - E1 = (-1/6 + 1/2) * (GMm/r) = (1/3) * (GMm/r) = +GMm / (3r).

A8 · Diagnose a tempting wrong idea

Tempting claim: To move a satellite into a higher orbit, you only need to supply potential energy.

Why it fails: Moving to a higher orbit requires increasing total energy E, but in the higher orbit the required circular speed is actually lower (v_o decreases as 1/sqrt(r)). Kinetic energy decreases, while potential energy increases by twice that amount.

Repair: Use total energy bookkeeping: Delta_E = Delta_K + Delta_U = -Delta_K. Potential energy increases by 2*|Delta_K|, so net energy required is +|Delta_K|.

A9 · Counterexample, limit and synthesis

Changed condition

What is the orbital speed of a satellite skimming the very surface of Earth (r = R)?

v_surface = sqrt(G*M / R) = sqrt(g0 * R) ≈ sqrt(9.8 * 6.4 * 10^6) ≈ 7.92 km/s (First Cosmic Velocity).

Independent exit check

Explain using energy conservation why atmospheric drag speeds up a low Earth satellite.

Reveal the reasoned exit answer

Atmospheric drag removes mechanical energy: Delta_E < 0. For circular orbits, Delta_E = -Delta_K. Therefore, Delta_K = -Delta_E > 0! Half of the lost potential energy goes into heating the air, while the other half converts into increased orbital kinetic energy.
