Variation of gravity with altitude and depth

A1 · Observe and ask

Gravitational acceleration reaches its peak value at the planet's surface. As you climb into the sky, gravity decreases steadily. Surprisingly, as you descend deep underground into a mine shaft, gravity also decreases, eventually dropping to absolute zero at the center of the Earth.

Learning target

Model the piecewise interior/exterior gravity curve g(r), contrasting linear interior falloff g proportional to r with inverse-square exterior attenuation g proportional to 1/r^2.

A3 · Construct the model

Outside the Earth (r > R, altitude h = r - R): g(h) = g0 * [R / (R + h)]^2 ≈ g0 * (1 - 2h/R) for h << R. Inside the Earth (r < R, depth d = R - r): by the shell theorem, the outer shell of thickness d exerts zero net gravitational force on the interior object. Only the inner sphere of radius r = R - d attracts it: M_enc = M * (r/R)^3. Therefore, g(d) = G * M_enc / r^2 = G * M * r / R^3 = g0 * (1 - d/R). Interior gravity decreases linearly with depth.

A4 · Derive and check the rule

Piecewise gravity field of uniform sphere: g(r) = g0 * (r/R) for r <= R, and g(r) = g0 * (R/r)^2 for r >= R. Dimensions: [g] = L T^-2. Validity: uniform density sphere. For altitude approximation g(h) ≈ g0 * (1 - 2h/R), condition h << R (h < 0.05R) must hold strictly.

Check the frame, sign convention, unit and applicability before accepting a numerical result. For a new model the original scientific warrant still requires independent review.

A6 · Three progressive worked examples

FOUNDATION

Question: Find gravitational acceleration at a depth d = R/2 below the surface of Earth.

Reasoned solution: Using interior formula: g(d) = g0 * (1 - d/R) = g0 * (1 - 0.5) = 0.5 * g0.

CONNECTED

Question: At what altitude h above the surface does gravitational acceleration drop to g0 / 4?

Reasoned solution: g(h) = g0 * [R / (R + h)]^2 = g0 / 4 ==> [R / (R + h)] = 1/2 ==> R + h = 2R ==> h = R.

ADVANCED

Question: At what altitude h is the percentage decrease in g equal to the percentage decrease in g at a depth d = 64 km? Take Earth radius R = 6400 km.

Reasoned solution: Fractional drop at depth: Delta_g/g = d / R. For small altitude h << R, fractional drop is Delta_g/g ≈ 2h / R. Equating the two: 2h / R = d / R ==> h = d / 2 = 64 / 2 = 32 km.

A7 · Worked anchor and independent check

Problem: An object has weight W0 at the Earth's surface. Compare its weight at altitude h = R with its weight at depth d = R/2.

Solution and check: At altitude h = R: g_alt = g0 * [R / (R + R)]^2 = g0 * (1/2)^2 = g0 / 4 ==> W_alt = W0 / 4 = 0.25 W0. At depth d = R/2: g_depth = g0 * (1 - (R/2)/R) = g0 / 2 ==> W_depth = W0 / 2 = 0.50 W0. The object is twice as heavy at depth R/2 as it is at altitude R.

A8 · Diagnose a tempting wrong idea

Tempting claim: The formula g(h) = g0 * (1 - 2h/R) can be used to find gravity at altitude h = R.

Why it fails: Using the approximation gives g(R) = g0 * (1 - 2*R/R) = -g0, which is physically absurd. The binomial expansion (1 + h/R)^-2 ≈ 1 - 2h/R is valid only when h/R << 1.

Repair: For significant altitudes (h >= 0.05R), always use the exact formula g(h) = g0 / (1 + h/R)^2. At h = R, exact g is g0 / (1 + 1)^2 = g0 / 4.

A9 · Counterexample, limit and synthesis

Changed condition

What is the gravitational field at the exact center of the Earth (r = 0)?

g(0) = g0 * (0 / R) = 0 N/kg. Gravitational attraction from all symmetric surrounding mass elements cancels out completely.

Independent exit check

A straight frictionless tunnel is bored through the center of the Earth. A ball is dropped from one end. What motion does it execute?

Reveal the reasoned exit answer

Inside the tunnel, the restoring force is F = -m * g(r) = -m * (g0 / R) * r = -k * r, where k = m*g0/R is a constant. This is Hooke's Law! The ball executes simple harmonic motion (SHM) with angular frequency omega = sqrt(g0 / R) and period T = 2*pi*sqrt(R/g0) ≈ 84.3 minutes.
