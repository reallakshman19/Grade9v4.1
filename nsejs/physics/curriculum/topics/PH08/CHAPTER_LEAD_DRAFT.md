# PH08 — Reflection of sound and echo travel time

**AUTHOR-EXPOSED CORE1A LEAD DRAFT · NOT COMPLETE CHAPTER · ACADEMIC HOLD**

## Begin with an observation

A person claps by a cliff and hears a later reflected copy of the sound.

## Build the physical idea

Sound is a disturbance that travels through matter. When a wave encounters a large reflecting surface, part of its energy travels back into the original region. The reflected pulse can reach a listener after the direct sound. That delay carries geometric information: it measures propagation along a path, not merely a straight-line distance printed on a picture. For a person standing beside the source and facing a large stationary wall, the same approximate distance is traversed outward and back. Therefore an echo delay is twice the one-way travel time. If the microphone is elsewhere, the path is source-to-wall plus wall-to-receiver. Echo questions become advanced when their representation changes, not when large numbers are inserted. We should teach reflections and timing diagrams before showing distance shortcuts.

## Derive the model, not a shortcut

1. **Draw and label the outward path and the reflected return path.** Why valid: Time-of-flight is defined along the actual traversed distance.

2. **Write total propagation time as the sum of each path length divided by speed.** Why valid: At constant speed a journey length L consumes time L/v; distinct legs add in time.

3. **Only for equal outgoing and incoming lengths, set total time to 2d/v and solve for d.** Why valid: The factor of two is a geometric consequence of the symmetric round trip, not a universal property of sound.

## Three worked tiers

### Foundation

**Question:** A stationary listener and source are 85 m from a wall; sound travels at 340 m/s. Find the delay.

**Reasoning:** The total path is 170 m, so the reflected sound returns after 0.50 s.

**Independent check:** Multiply 0.50 s by 340 m/s to recover 170 m traveled, not 85 m.

### Connected

**Question:** An echo delay of 0.80 s is recorded with colocated source and receiver; v=330 m/s. Estimate the wall distance.

**Reasoning:** The round-trip distance is 264 m; the one-way wall distance is 132 m.

**Independent check:** The check is 132/330 + 132/330 = 0.80 s.

### Advanced

**Question:** A source is 30 m from the reflector and a microphone 50 m from it along the same ray line on the source side. Find the reflected travel time at 320 m/s.

**Reasoning:** The two legs total 80 m, yielding 0.25 s; the 2d shortcut does not apply.

**Independent check:** Recalculate from both legs separately: 30/320 + 50/320 = 0.25 s.

## Conditions and limitations

- Distance equals vt/2 only for an outward-and-back path of equal length
- A separate microphone or off-axis reflector changes the path sum
- Distinct audibility depends on delay, intensity and environmental conditions

## Diagnose a plausible mistake

**Wrong idea:** Every echo distance is simply sound speed multiplied by delay.

**Diagnostic:** A microphone receives a reflection after 0.30 s. Does that always mean the obstacle is 102 m away at 340 m/s?

**Repair:** Trace the exact source-reflector-receiver path; the division by two applies only to the colocated round trip.

## Exit question

A colocated source and detector record an echo at 0.60 s in air at 340 m/s. How far is the wall?

**Answer:** 102 m. The sound travels a total of 204 m: 102 m outward and 102 m back.
