Longitudinal waves: displacement, compression and pressure graphs

Phenomenon

A compression can move along a row of air particles while each particle only oscillates around its equilibrium position.

Scientific construction

In a longitudinal wave, particles oscillate parallel to the direction of propagation. A compression is a region where particles are temporarily closer together than in their equilibrium arrangement; a rarefaction is a region where they are farther apart. In a sound wave, the disturbance and energy propagate through a medium while particles move locally. A diagram with a wavy line above a horizontal axis need not depict transverse particle motion: it may show the value of a longitudinal displacement or pressure field as a function of position.

Imagine equilibrium positions x₁ and x₂ separated by Δx. At a snapshot, their positions become x₁+ξ₁ and x₂+ξ₂. Their new separation is Δx+ξ₂−ξ₁. If the second displacement is smaller than the first, the spacing decreases and the region is compressed. If the second displacement is larger, the spacing increases and it is rarefied. If both displacements are equal, their separation is unchanged even if both have moved far from equilibrium. Thus a displacement maximum is not automatically a compression maximum.

For a small smooth displacement field ξ(x), the fractional change in spacing is approximately ∂ξ/∂x. Conservation of mass gives fractional density change approximately −∂ξ/∂x; in a linear acoustic model excess pressure is proportional to compression, p′=−B∂ξ/∂x, where B is the appropriate bulk modulus. These derivative relations are optional authored enrichment; the finite-neighbour argument is sufficient for qualitative reasoning. A sinusoidal displacement crest has locally zero gradient; compression peaks near the downward zero crossing and rarefaction near the upward crossing, given the stated coordinate convention. The sign of a displacement itself does not tell whether neighbouring particles are compressed.

A spatial graph at one time describes field values along x. A time trace at one fixed detector describes how one measured field varies with time. Their slopes have different units and meanings. In a progressive sinusoidal wave the time trace may be related to the spatial trace if direction and wave speed are known. Do not infer spatial compression directly from a temporal displacement slope without the travelling-wave model and direction. For a right-moving profile ξ(x−vt), the temporal derivative equals −v times the spatial derivative; reversing direction changes the sign relation.

When constructing a graph, label equilibrium coordinate, particle displacement or excess pressure, and physical units. Draw the actual positions of a few particles to check the inferred compression. In an inverse problem, a pressure snapshot constrains differences in displacement, not the common offset of every displacement; a boundary or displacement reference supplies missing information. A uniform offset added to all positions leaves spacing unchanged. This is a model ambiguity, not bulk transport by the sound wave. Build the model

  1. Mark equilibrium particle positions and their signed longitudinal displacements. Why: Actual position is equilibrium position plus displacement.
  2. Calculate changed adjacent spacing rather than compare displacement magnitudes. Why: Compression corresponds to reduced spacing and increased density.
  3. Translate spacing changes into spatial displacement slope and qualitative pressure. Why: Negative slope gives compression under the stated coordinate convention.
  4. Test inverse or temporal interpretation with direction and reference data. Why: Only spatial differences determine spacing; boundary and propagation information can resolve ambiguity.

Worked examples

FOUNDATION

Particles at 0 and 10 mm have displacements +1 and −1 mm. Find actual spacing.

8 mm, compression.

Independent check: Actual positions 1 and 9 mm.

CONNECTED

Four equilibrium positions 0,10,20,30 mm have displacements 0,+1,+1,0 mm. Classify intervals.

Actual 0,11,21,30; spacings 11,10,9 mm: rarefaction, unchanged, compression.

Independent check: Displacement maximum alone did not fix compression.

ADVANCED

A spatial displacement trace has equal upward and downward zero crossings. Where are pressure extrema in the ideal sinusoidal model?

Downward spatial crossing corresponds to compression/high pressure, upward crossing to rarefaction/low pressure.

Independent check: Use sign of −spatial slope, not sign of displacement.

Misconception and repair

A displacement crest is a compression crest, and the wavy graph is a transverse particle path.

If adjacent particles have the same positive displacement, has their separation decreased?

Calculate actual spacing; distinguish field value, spatial gradient and direction of physical particle motion.

Limits

  • Small longitudinal displacements and locally smooth field for derivative/linear pressure model.
  • Neighbour labels refer to equilibrium positions; displacements must not imply particle crossing.
  • Pressure and displacement traces are distinct variables; spatial and temporal slopes not interchangeable.
  • Ideal uniform offset ambiguity is a snapshot relation; physical boundary conditions and dynamics may constrain it.

Exit check

Equilibrium positions 0, 10, 20 mm have displacements +1, +2, 0 mm. Classify spacings.

Check reasoning

11 mm rarefaction, 8 mm compression. Actual positions 1,12,20 mm.
