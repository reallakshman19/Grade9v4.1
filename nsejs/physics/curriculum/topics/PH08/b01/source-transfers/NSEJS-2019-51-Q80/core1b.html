Provisional MAIN placeholder

1 · Predict

Predict whether a larger microphone reading requires different fundamental frequency.

2 · Attempt

Two sounding boards carry identical strings whose measured periodic motions have equal frequency and equal displacement amplitude. One board produces a calibrated microphone pressure amplitude twice the other at the same location in the same air. A report states the strings must have different frequencies and the louder board's wave must have twice the wavelength. Evaluate the claims, construct a consistent alternative and identify measurements that could test it.

Author-exposed reconstruction, diagnosis and repair

3 · Reconstruct

Same measured f and same air speed imply same airborne λ; changed coupling may double pressure without frequency change. Under matched linear progressive conditions intensity ratio4, not automatically perceived loudness ratio. Measure source motion, air speed, calibrated pressure and angular/field response.

4 · Diagnose

Identify confusion between source displacement, sound pressure, intensity and loudness, or between shared frequency and wavelength.

5 · Repair

Label each medium and measured variable, calculate λ separately, compare calibrated intensities only under stated impedance and field assumptions.

6 · Boundary test

If the microphones are in different gases with unknown impedances, can pressure ratio alone establish intensity ratio4?

Boundary criterion (answer)

No; I_A/I_B=4Z_B/Z_A under stated acoustic model.

Fresh INSTANCE

1 · Predict

Predict which of the two claims, if either, follows from a common source frequency.

2 · Attempt

A string wave and radiated airborne tone have common frequency 250 Hz. String speed125 m/s; air speed350 m/s. Two configurations use the same string displacement amplitude but different sounding bodies, with pressure amplitudes 3 and 6 Pa in matched progressive air fields. A student claims the wavelengths are equal and the second configuration is four times as loud. Assess both claims and state a defensible quantitative conclusion.

Author-exposed reconstruction, diagnosis and repair

3 · Reconstruct

String λ=0.5 m, air λ=1.4 m, not equal. Same impedance gives intensity ratio4; loudness ratio not established. Coupling can account for pressure difference without string displacement change.

4 · Diagnose

Identify confusion between source displacement, sound pressure, intensity and loudness, or between shared frequency and wavelength.

5 · Repair

Label each medium and measured variable, calculate λ separately, compare calibrated intensities only under stated impedance and field assumptions.

6 · Boundary test

If the air speed becomes125 m/s while f stays250 Hz, what changes in the wavelength claim?

Boundary criterion (answer)

Both wavelengths become0.5 m under given models, but equality is conditional on matching wave speed and frequency.

Publisher-locator review (answer exposure)

NSEJS-2019-51-Q80, p.18: Publisher paper · Publisher final key. Transverse motion of string and airborne longitudinal sound differ; common frequency does not imply equal wavelength. Publisher key D accepts an amplitude-squared loudness assertion; physically, intensity scales with pressure amplitude squared under matched impedance, while perceived loudness is not generally proportional and string-to-air coupling matters.
