Scientific construction

For two tones in a nondispersive medium, v=f₁λ₁=f₂λ₂. Let r=f₂/f₁>1; then λ₁−λ₂=v(1/f₁−1/f₂)=v(r−1)/(rf₁). The lower frequency has the larger wavelength. Infer speed only if both tones share it; a changed medium or dispersion breaks that constraint. A measured period gives f=1/T, with milliseconds converted to seconds. A third measured wavelength can test the common-speed hypothesis. This source-driven lesson uses distinct original cases.

Worked foundation

Two tones have periods10 and5 ms in a medium of speed300 m/s. Find wavelength difference.

f=100,200 Hz; λ=3,1.5 m; difference1.5 m.

Worked construction

Two tones have f=120 and180 Hz and wavelength difference0.9 m. Find common speed and verify a measured third tone at90 Hz has λ=3.6 m.

v=324 m/s; wavelengths2.7 and1.8 m; third λ=3.6 m, consistent.

Limits and repair

Specified ideal or matched conditions only; supplied quantitative relations do not establish compulsory exam scope. Locate any inverted ratio, ms conversion or unjustified shared-speed assumption. Write the two v=fλ equations, subtract wavelength expressions and check each recovered product.
