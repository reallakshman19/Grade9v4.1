Build the idea

In a stationary uniform medium, a subsonic source moving toward a stationary wall emits wavefronts at intervals T_s=1/f_s. Ahead of it the spacing is (v−u)T_s, behind it (v+u)T_s. A pulse emitted at initial wall distance d takes d/v to reach the wall. Its reflected path then meets the moving receiver on the source. If source and receiver remain colocated and approach at speed u, total propagation distance is 2d−ut, so t=2d/(v+u), provided reflection and reception occur before wall contact. A stationary wall reflects the incident frequency in the medium's frame without shifting it. Ahead-of-source frequency at the wall is v f_s/(v−u); for the reflected fronts moving back toward the approaching receiver, encounter rate is (v+u) divided by the forward spacing. Thus f_echo=(v+u)f_s/(v−u). The echo delay is not 2d/v for a moving receiver. These are optional ideal-model extensions; real airflow, motion changes, reflection geometry and source bandwidth require additional constraints. A source at rest has reference wavelength v/f_s; that is not the forward spacing of a moving source.

Worked foundation

A moving acoustic cart is 90 m from a wall, travels toward it at10 m/s, v=350 m/s, f_s=200 Hz. Predict first pulse return and forward spacing.

t=180/360=0.5 s; receiver advances5 m and remains85 m away. Forward spacing340/200=1.7 m; stationary-source reference1.75 m.

Worked reasoning construction

A source/receiver approaches a wall at20 m/s from160 m, v=320 m/s and f_s=400 Hz. Derive first echo time and returned frequency, compare with an independent echo frequency450 Hz.

t=320/340=16/17 s; λ_ahead=300/400=0.75 m. Reflected encounter frequency340/0.75=1360/3≈453.33 Hz. Exact450 Hz is inconsistent with the ideal model, not proof of a unique cause.

Model limits and wrong route

Stationary medium, uniform subsonic speed, collinear fixed wall, specified ideal reflection.

Check whether your diagram uses the receiver position at emission instead of reception, or whether you used v/f_s as moving-source spacing. Draw emission, reflection and reception events; solve the meeting equation in the medium frame, then derive front spacing from successive emission positions and check the reflected encounter rate.
