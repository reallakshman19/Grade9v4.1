Scientific construction

In a linear small-amplitude model, disturbances of the same physical quantity at the same position and time superpose by signed addition. Do not add magnitudes or multiply waveforms. Pressure perturbations add to pressure perturbations, not to displacement in metres. A resultant trace can be smaller than either component due to destructive interference; zero resultant at one instant or position does not show that each wave vanishes or that energy is destroyed. For an inverse problem R=A+B, B=R−A pointwise if A and R are measured on the same scale with synchronized clocks. If both components are unknown, infinitely many pairs can fit the resultant. For sampled piecewise-constant signals define each time interval and use actual signed values, not merely graph shape.

Worked foundation

At four equal time bins, A=[2,−1,1,0] Pa and B=[−2,3,0,−1] Pa. Construct resultant.

R=[0,2,1,−1] Pa. First bin cancellation, not no component.

Worked reasoning construction

Measured R=[1,−2,3,0] Pa and component A=[−1,1,2,−2] Pa. Reconstruct B and verify.

B=[2,−3,1,2] Pa; add bin by bin. Without A, R alone is not a unique decomposition.

Model limits and misconception

Only specified ideal/matched conditions; extension relations are supplied, not independently warranted compulsory syllabus scope. Check bin alignment, signed rather than magnitude addition, and physical units. Tabulate A, B and R with one row per interval; subtract the known component, add back, and redraw the aligned step traces.
