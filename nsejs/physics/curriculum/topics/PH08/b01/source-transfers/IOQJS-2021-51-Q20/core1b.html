1 · Predict

Predict whether a decreasing envelope requires decreasing time between corresponding maxima.

2 · Attempt

A linear oscillator of unchanged spring stiffness has measured positive extrema at 1,6,11,16 ms with amplitudes12,9,6,3 mm. Draw the sampled displacement extrema and envelope, tabulate consecutive full periods and normalized energies at these turning points. A report claims falling amplitude proves increasing frequency. Assess it.

Author-exposed reconstruction, diagnosis and repair

3 · Reconstruct

Full periods5 ms each, f200 Hz. E/E₁=1,9/16,1/4,1/16. Draw extrema on same time axis; negative extrema unspecified, so do not invent exact intervening waveform. Claim contradicted by equal peak spacing.

4 · Diagnose

Check peak type, time spacing, amplitude normalization and source versus detector variable.

5 · Repair

Align time axes, compute full-period intervals and energy proxy only at specified extrema, then test source/detector alternatives.

6 · Boundary test

If an independent spring-stiffness measurement changes between trials, is A² alone enough for comparing stored energy?

Boundary response criterion

No; energy at turning point depends on kA²/2.

6 · Boundary test

If receiver gain is independently shown to halve between first and second peaks, what is second normalized physical pressure amplitude relative to first?

Boundary response criterion

Observed .4 divided by gain factor .5 gives .8, conditional on linear detector and matched calibration.
