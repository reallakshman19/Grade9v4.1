Build the idea

The time to reach a common thermal endpoint is E divided by net energy accumulation rate. With two independently controlled ideal power sources A and B, their measured input powers add if their operation does not alter each other's electrical input. If loss is negligible, reciprocal endpoint times add. If a common fixed outward loss L acts once on the whole system, then 1/t_A=(P_A−L)/E, 1/t_B=(P_B−L)/E and 1/t_AB=(P_A+P_B−L)/E. Adding the two single-source net rates counts the same loss twice. The useful joint rate is their sum plus L. A changed joint-operation loss must be treated as another parameter, not silently assigned to either source. An independently measured energy requirement and electrical input make a loss hypothesis testable. Equal temperature endpoints alone do not guarantee equal energy requirements if mass, phase or capacity changes.

Worked foundation

A simulator needs 12000 J. Inputs A=100 W, B=80 W and fixed loss20 W. Calculate single and joint times.

A useful80 W →150 s; B60 W →200 s; joint160 W →75 s. 1/150+1/200=7/600, not 1/75=8/600 because the common loss is counted once.

An unchanged-phase target needs18000 J. Inputs A=100 W and B=140 W. A alone reaches target in225 s; joint operation reaches it in90 s. B alone is an unused trial. Fit a common loss from A, test it against joint and predict B; an observed B time150 s is then provided.

Model limits and wrong route

Hypothetical model only; matched energy endpoints, positive net power, constant stated parameters and no unaccounted phase conversion.

Locate where you added useful rates as though each independent trial's loss were a separate simultaneous drain, or where you accepted a model without checking both single trials. Convert each endpoint time into net energy rate, infer each loss from independent input, test the model's equality constraints and reserve a prediction.
