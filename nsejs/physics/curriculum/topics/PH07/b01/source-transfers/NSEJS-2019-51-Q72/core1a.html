Scientific construction

For a floating object in equilibrium, displaced liquid mass equals object mass (neglect air buoyancy). In a vertical-sided vessel, the volume below the water surface equals the initial liquid volume plus the submerged displacement. If pure floating ice melts completely into the same water and no mass is lost, the final liquid mass is the sum of original liquid and ice mass. At the same liquid density and unchanged vessel geometry, the final level is unchanged: the melt replaces exactly its displaced volume. Water is unusual: pure liquid water near ordinary pressure has maximum density near 4 °C. Cooling it below that temperature reduces density and raises volume at fixed mass. If initially at 4 °C and the final mixture is colder, the level may rise; if allowed to return to 4 °C it need not. A full thermal prediction requires initial ice temperature, masses, heat input and heat losses; no final temperature follows merely from melting. For quantitative reasoning use supplied density data and a rigid constant-area vessel.

Worked foundation

A 100 g ice piece floats in 900 g water with density1 g/cm³. After melting at the same density, find initial and final volume below surface.

Initial 900+100=1000 cm³; final 1000/1=1000 cm³. Level unchanged.

Worked reasoning construction

A rigid 100 cm² beaker holds 600 g water and 200 g floating pure ice. Initial liquid density1.000 g/cm³; final liquid density0.998 g/cm³. Find level change and compare same-density model.

Initial800 cm³; final800/0.998≈801.603 cm³, rise≈0.01603 cm=0.1603 mm. Same density predicts zero.
