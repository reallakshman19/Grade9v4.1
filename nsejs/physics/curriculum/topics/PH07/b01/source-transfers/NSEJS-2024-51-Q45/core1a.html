Build the idea

The input to a thermal system is energy per time. If an ideal resistor has resistance R=ρL/A, at fixed potential difference V its electrical input is V²/R. For a circular uniform wire A=πD²/4, so comparing otherwise identical material wires gives R₂/R₁=(L₂/L₁)/(D₂/D₁)². At fixed current instead, P=I²R. The circuit constraint changes the scaling. In an unchanged-phase system with a prescribed energy requirement E and a constant outward loss P_loss, the useful rate is P_in−P_loss. For positive useful power, t=E/(P_in−P_loss). These equations are supplied here as cross-topic model relations; this is not a practical heater-construction or mains-wiring exercise. A constant loss is an idealization, not a general heating law. A common endpoint requires matched mass, initial temperature and final state. At nonpositive net power the stipulated endpoint cannot be reached by positive accumulation.

Worked foundation

A hypothetical thermal simulator requires 18 kJ to reach its target. Its ideal input is 120 W and loss 20 W. A second simulated resistor has twice the length and twice the area of the first, supplied at the same voltage. Compare useful power and time.

Resistance unchanged because L/A unchanged; both have 120 W input, 100 W useful, and 180 s heating time. Confirm by 100×180=18000 J.

Worked reasoning construction

An ideal model has input 90 W and loss 15 W, target energy 9000 J. A replacement model has resistance 3/4 of original at voltage 2/3 of original. Determine whether target can be reached and compare times.

P₂/P₁=(2/3)²/(3/4)=16/27; P₂=160/3 W; net=115/3 W; t₂=5400/23 s≈234.78 s; t₁=120 s. Both positive. Check net power times time equals 9000 J.

Model limits and wrong route

Hypothetical model only; matched energy endpoints, positive net power, constant stated parameters and no unaccounted phase conversion.

Identify whether you scaled diameter as area, used a fixed-current formula for fixed voltage, or omitted loss from the energy budget. Write separate geometry, electrical input and thermal net-power ratios. Recompute the endpoint energy check.
