Temperature scale calibration: absolute readings, interval and precision

Scientific construction

An affine scale is r=aθ+b, where θ is Celsius temperature and r is the readout. Two distinct reference temperatures determine a and b if the linear model applies. Subtract readings to remove the offset; use a third independent reference to test the assumption. Kelvin temperature is T=θ+273.15, but ΔT in kelvin equals Δθ in degrees Celsius. A conversion does not create measurement precision. A reading rounded to 0.1 °C has at least the stated rounding limitation; calibration and sensor response can add uncertainty. A systematic offset can leave a measured change correct while making absolute readings wrong.

Worked foundation

A sensor reads 8 and 68 at 10 °C and 40 °C. Find its affine relation and predicted readout at 25 °C.

Slope 2 units/°C; offset −12; predicted 38. An offset-only model cannot fit the two points.

Worked reasoning construction

Two calibrations at 5 and45 °C read 20 and100. A reserved 25 °C reference reads 62. Test the affine model; if a sample reads 80, give conditional Celsius and Kelvin estimates.

r=2θ+10; predicted60 not62; exact model fails. Conditional sample θ=35 °C, T=308.15 K, not certified.

Model limits and misconception

Only specified ideal/matched conditions; extension relations are supplied, not independently warranted compulsory syllabus scope. Identify whether you treated a scale factor as an offset, used 273.15 for a temperature change, or ignored a failed check. Write separate equations, eliminate the offset, check an unused reference, and label any derived endpoints conditional.
