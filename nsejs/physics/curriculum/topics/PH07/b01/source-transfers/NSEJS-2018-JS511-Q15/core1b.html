Provisional MAIN placeholder

1 · Predict

Predict whether ordinary visible color alone is enough to rank emitted brightness at a different temperature.

2 · Attempt

Two equal-area model surfaces are observed at the same known high temperature in darkness. A is dark in ordinary illumination, B is pale. A report concludes A must emit more visible light and more total radiation. Construct two compatible spectral emissivity scenarios that make the visible ranking differ; specify independent evidence needed for total-power ranking.

Author-exposed reconstruction, diagnosis and repair

3 · Reconstruct

Ordinary reflected color alone does not bind high-temperature spectral values. Scenario1 visible emissivity A=.8 B=.3; scenario2 A=.3 B=.8 at the operating state; both can be compatible with low-temperature color labels absent stability assumptions. Measure operating-temperature spectral emissivities or calibrated emitted spectrum, control area, T and background; total-power ranking needs full relevant spectrum.

4 · Diagnose

Identify where you replaced spectral measurement by a color label or confused radiation with total energy loss.

5 · Repair

State the operating band, temperature, emissivity and comparison conditions; provide compatible alternative and a discriminator.

6 · Boundary test

If both are explicitly gray surfaces with stable emissivities .8 and .3, same area and T hotter than surroundings, what follows?

Boundary response criterion

A has greater modeled net radiative power; ratio8/3 if surroundings and geometry matched.

Fresh INSTANCE

1 · Predict

Predict whether higher infrared radiance alone establishes faster cooling.

2 · Attempt

A thermal-imaging model reports two panels at equal temperature and geometry. Panel X has higher measured infrared radiance than Y in the sensor band. A student claims X is visibly brighter and necessarily cools faster. Assess both claims and propose a test that separates emissivity from other energy-loss paths.

Author-exposed reconstruction, diagnosis and repair

3 · Reconstruct

IR-band ordering alone does not fix visible emissivity or total loss; cooling also depends on thermal capacity, conduction, convection and full spectral emission. Measure visible-band radiance and matched-state steady power or heat capacity plus loss-path controls.

4 · Diagnose

Identify where you replaced spectral measurement by a color label or confused radiation with total energy loss.

5 · Repair

State the operating band, temperature, emissivity and comparison conditions; provide compatible alternative and a discriminator.

6 · Boundary test

If total net radiative power is independently greater for X but X has twice the thermal capacity, must its temperature initially fall faster?

Boundary response criterion

No; compare P_loss/C, including other paths.
