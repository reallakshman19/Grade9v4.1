# PH12 — Qualitative electromagnetic induction and changing field linkage

**AUTHOR-EXPOSED CORE1A LEAD DRAFT · NOT COMPLETE CHAPTER · ACADEMIC HOLD**

## Begin with an observation

A magnet pushed toward a coil can make a connected meter deflect, while the same magnet held still may not.

## Build the physical idea

Magnetic effects and induction reverse a familiar cause-and-effect relationship: a current can create a magnetic field, but a changing magnetic linkage can also produce an electrical response. The word 'changing' is essential. Holding a magnet stationary beside a fixed loop provides magnetic flux, but not a continuing induced EMF in the ideal qualitative model. Pushing it toward the loop changes the linkage; pulling it away reverses the trend. A closed loop can carry a transient induced current. For direction, the response tends to oppose the change in linkage rather than simply oppose every magnetic field. Avoid declaring a clockwise current without first specifying the viewpoint and the orientation of the magnet. In Class X the motor, generator and induction components should retain their formative curriculum status, not be promoted by this chapter into an advanced compulsory Faraday-law calculation.

## Derive the model, not a shortcut

1. **Specify the loop, magnetic field direction and whether linked flux is increasing or decreasing.** Why valid: Magnetic field magnitude at one instant does not reveal whether the linkage is changing.

2. **Use the qualitative induction principle to decide whether an induced electrical response occurs.** Why valid: Change of linkage is the physically decisive event; the current path depends on electrical continuity.

3. **For a closed circuit and an explicit viewpoint, apply opposition to the flux change to determine the induced field and possible current direction.** Why valid: Lenz-type direction is a response to the flux change, so the viewpoint and field orientation are needed.

## Three worked tiers

### Foundation

**Question:** Move the north pole of a bar magnet toward a fixed closed coil. Should a momentary meter deflection be expected?

**Reasoning:** Yes, under adequate flux coupling, because linked flux changes as the magnet approaches.

**Independent check:** Hold the magnet still: the ideal transient deflection ceases.

### Connected

**Question:** Open the coil circuit while the magnet approaches; compare current with the closed-coil case.

**Reasoning:** A changing linkage can still produce an induced EMF, but no sustained current flows through an open circuit.

**Independent check:** Differentiate an induced potential from current through a closed conducting loop.

### Advanced

**Question:** Reverse the magnet's motion while keeping orientation unchanged; predict the sign of response.

**Reasoning:** The sign of the rate of change reverses, so the induced response reverses in a consistently fixed measurement convention.

**Independent check:** State the observation side before claiming clockwise or anticlockwise direction.

## Conditions and limitations

- A stationary field and stationary closed circuit do not by themselves imply sustained induced current
- Induced current requires a closed conducting path; an open loop may exhibit an induced potential without current
- Quantitative Faraday-rate formulas and motional-emf terminal velocity are enrichment beyond this formative treatment

## Diagnose a plausible mistake

**Wrong idea:** A strong stationary magnet beside a stationary loop must generate a continuous current.

**Diagnostic:** If the magnet is held still after insertion, does a continuing meter deflection follow merely from field strength?

**Repair:** No. The standard induction effect depends on change of flux linkage, and current also requires a closed path.

## Exit question

A fixed magnet stays inside a closed stationary coil. In an ideal qualitative experiment, is there continued induction?

**Answer:** No continuing induced EMF from an unchanging linkage. A changing magnetic flux, not the mere presence of magnetic field, is required.
