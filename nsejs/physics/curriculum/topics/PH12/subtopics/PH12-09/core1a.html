Induced emf versus induced current under changing linkage

Physical question and assumptions

Conducting loop in a changing magnetic flux configuration; qualitative electromagnetic induction; do not equate an open circuit's induced emf with sustained circulating current.

What you must already understand

  • Magnetic field and oriented loop
  • Closed current paths
  • Change versus static field

Build the model, don't memorize the answer

A change in magnetic flux linkage produces an induced electromotive response; a closed conducting loop permits circulating current if resistance and other conditions allow. A strong stationary field with unchanged linkage does not imply sustained induction.

Worked Core1A anchor (different from the later independent challenge)

A bar magnet approaches a stationary closed loop, is held still, and then is withdrawn along the same axis. Predict the qualitative meter response across each stage.

  1. During approach, linkage changes and an induced current can flow.
  2. While magnet and loop are held stationary, linkage is unchanged and the induction response ceases.
  3. During withdrawal, linkage changes oppositely and the current direction reverses relative to approach, with consistent magnet orientation.

Independent physical consistency check: Response depends on flux change rather than field strength alone; direction claims require a specified observer and pole.

Misconception and corrective question

A magnetic field itself causes continuous induction even when flux linkage does not change.

Repair prompt: Which physical condition and representation must be checked before applying the result? Explain why a result that happens to match one example may fail when an assumption changes.
