Return-path retracing and competing optical configurations

Subtopic textbookFresh Core1B

Reasoning goal

Determine whether the stated all-ray normal incidence and retracing can hold, not just whether the central ray returns.

Scientific construction

A thin converging lens sends rays from its axial focal point out parallel to the axis. A perpendicular plane mirror returns these rays along their paths regardless of its axial position (assuming finite aperture does not clip). A different case can occur if a mirror lies at a real image plane: rays converging to the mirror point are not generally normal, but the plane reflection changes the bundle and exact retracing of every oblique ray is not guaranteed. Distinguish axial central ray from all paraxial rays.

Worked anchor

F=10 cm, object p=10 cm, lens output collimated, perpendicular mirror at x=25 cm: reflected rays retrace and return to x=−10 cm through lens. For p=15 cm, outgoing rays converge toward x=30 cm; off-axis rays at the mirror are not normal.

Common wrong route

Inferring all-ray retracing from the central ray or a conjugate focus at the mirror.

Trace at least two off-axis rays; test their incident directions against the mirror normal.

Model boundary

What if the lens or mirror aperture clips the collimated bundle?
