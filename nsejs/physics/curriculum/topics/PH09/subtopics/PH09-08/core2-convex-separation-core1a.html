Convex mirror image and separation as distinct observables

Subtopic textbookFresh Core1B

Reasoning goal

Model signed object–image separation, distinguish it from image position and magnification, and use independent observations to test mirror parameters.

Scientific construction

For a real object distance d>0 and convex mirror focal magnitude F>0, v=Fd/(F+d), m=F/(F+d), and object–image separation S=d+v. The two distances must not be subtracted because object and image lie on opposite sides. For d₂>d₁, v₂−v₁=F²(d₂−d₁)/[(F+d₂)(F+d₁)]>0, and S₂−S₁=(d₂−d₁)+(v₂−v₁)>0. A separation measurement does not by itself equal the image position.

Worked anchor

F=12 cm, object d=24 cm then d=12 cm: v=8 then 6 cm; S=32 then 18 cm. Image grows from m=1/3 to 1/2 while the separation shrinks. No contradiction.

Common wrong route

Subtracting the image coordinate from the positive object distance, or treating magnification as separation.

Mark signed coordinates u=−d and v>0, then write S=v−u; use m=v/d separately.

Model boundary

As d approaches zero from above, what are the limiting values of S and m, and why is this only an ideal paraxial limit?
