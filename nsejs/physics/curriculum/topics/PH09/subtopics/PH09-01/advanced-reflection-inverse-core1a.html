Inverse reflection directions

AUTHOR_EXPOSED · ACADEMIC HOLD

Original teaching and transfer; no qualified direct examination source for this construction. Independent QRT and source review HOLD.
Subtopic textbookFresh Core1B

Reasoning goal

Reconstruct a common reflecting-line orientation from multiple directed ray observations, then test whether a finite mirror can realize the observed paths.

Scientific construction

In a fixed plane, a reflecting line with tangent heading φ maps incident heading θ to θ′=2φ−θ modulo 360°. The inverse tangent is (θ+θ′)/2 modulo 180°. A line direction alone does not establish a hit on its finite reflecting face; the intersection and ray parameter must be checked.

Worked anchor

An incoming direction 20° becomes 100°: φ=60° modulo 180°. Rotating this mirror by 15° with the incident direction fixed rotates the outgoing direction by 30°. A normal-incidence ray reverses direction.

Common wrong route

Checking only equal angles without checking which side rays arrive from.

Draw vectors and reflect them; separately test signed distances from the mirror line for each incident origin.

Model boundary

Can a ray with heading parallel to the mirror line be treated as an ordinary point reflection? Explain.
