Reasoning goal

Convert measurements relative to focus and pole into one signed position-line representation and use it to identify inconsistent experimental claims.

Scientific construction

For a convex mirror, focus at +F, pole at 0, real object at −d and virtual image at +v. Object-to-focus magnitude y=F+d, image-to-focus magnitude z=F−v. The paraxial mirror equation gives v=Fd/(F+d), so z=F²/(F+d)=F²/y. The distance from the focus is not the distance from pole. With radius magnitude R, F≈R/2 under paraxial approximation.

Worked anchor

R=40 cm, F=20 cm and y=60 cm gives d=40 cm,z=400/60=6.67 cm,v=13.33 cm. Check the order object,pole,image,focus.

Common wrong route

Using object-to-focus or image-to-focus magnitude as u or v without moving origin.

Draw all points in one fixed axis, then derive y=d+F,z=F−v before using Newton relation.

Model boundary

For a finite real object, can the image lie beyond the convex mirror focus? Use the same position line.
