11. Mirror magnification and measurement

Linear magnification is the ratio of image height to object height with sign encoding orientation under a declared convention. For paraxial spherical mirrors m=-v/u. Interpreting its sign requires the axis convention, while interpreting its magnitude requires both heights to be measured consistently.

A classroom concave-mirror focal-length experiment can place a screen near the focus when the target is sufficiently distant. But a nearby source does not supply truly parallel rays, and the screen position has uncertainty. Therefore a single sharpness estimate is an approximation rather than an exact measurement of f.

Build and check the model

Experimental mirror predictions need a declared measurement origin, a controlled object, and a recorded sharpness criterion. For concave mirror real conjugates, F=dq/(d+q) where d and q are positive magnitudes. Repeated measurements should give a consistent F within uncertainty; a systematic offset at the pole or finite aperture can bias results. A sign convention error changes the model itself, not just precision.

Worked anchor

d=30 cm, q=60 cm implies F=20 cm; d=40, q=40 also gives 20. If q is read from a stand 2 cm behind the pole, recorded q may be too large by 2 cm and bias inferred F.

Condition and limiting check

A broad or ambiguous focus is evidence to inspect aperture, alignment and object distance, not automatically a broken reflection law.

Misconception repair

Distinguish random scatter from a systematic datum or model error.

Self-check before transfer

Sketch the relevant geometry with arrows, state the applicable law and its assumptions, and verify the result using the condition above. The next page contains fresh authored tasks, not a reproduction of the worked anchor.
