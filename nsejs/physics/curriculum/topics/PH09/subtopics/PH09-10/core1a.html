11. Mirror magnification and measurement

Linear magnification is the ratio of image height to object height with sign encoding orientation under a declared convention. For paraxial spherical mirrors m=-v/u. Interpreting its sign requires the axis convention, while interpreting its magnitude requires both heights to be measured consistently.

A classroom concave-mirror focal-length experiment can place a screen near the focus when the target is sufficiently distant. But a nearby source does not supply truly parallel rays, and the screen position has uncertainty. Therefore a single sharpness estimate is an approximation rather than an exact measurement of f.

Build and check the model

Magnification is a ratio of signed heights, not image distance itself. For a paraxial spherical mirror, similar triangles at P give h_i/h_o=−v/u. A distant real object can estimate a concave mirror focal distance by focusing a sharp image on a screen; for finite d, |v|=Fd/(d−F)>F. The estimate overstates F if the object is not sufficiently distant.

Worked anchor

A concave mirror with F=10 cm focuses an object d=50 cm at |v|=12.5 cm. A 4 cm object gives a 1 cm inverted image: m=−0.25. At d=500 cm, |v|≈10.20 cm, closer to F.

Condition and limiting check

Do not use the Sun for a school focusing experiment without a safe, supervised method; use a distant terrestrial target or a collimated low-power source.

Misconception repair

A ruler measurement of image distance is not automatically focal length.
