# PH09 — Convex mirrors: virtual images, magnification and motion

**AUTHOR-EXPOSED CORE1A LEAD DRAFT · NOT COMPLETE CHAPTER · ACADEMIC HOLD**

## Begin with an observation

A vehicle approaching a convex side mirror appears upright and relatively small.

## Build the physical idea

A convex mirror spreads reflected paraxial rays. The eye interprets them by projecting the diverging directions backward, locating a virtual image behind the mirror even though no light converges at that location. To reason mathematically, mark the pole and positive axis before substituting signed distances. For a real object at u=-x (x positive) and a convex mirror of focal length f>0, the mirror equation produces v=fx/(x+f), a positive image position behind the mirror. The magnification -v/u is positive but smaller than one. As the object approaches, the apparent image moves toward the pole. The location is a consequence of a sign-consistent model and must agree with the ray drawing.

## Derive the model, not a shortcut

1. **Use the reflected rays and backward extensions to locate the virtual image.** Why valid: Diverging reflected rays do not physically cross behind the mirror; only their extensions do.

2. **State u=-x, f>0 and the mirror relation 1/f=1/v+1/u.** Why valid: The formula is valid for the paraxial spherical mirror with a consistent Cartesian convention.

3. **Solve for v=fx/(x+f) and obtain m=v/x=f/(x+f).** Why valid: With x>0 and f>0 the image coordinate v is positive and m lies between 0 and 1.

## Three worked tiers

### Foundation

**Question:** A convex mirror has f=12 cm and a real object 24 cm in front. Locate the image.

**Reasoning:** Set u=-24 cm; 1/12=1/v-1/24, giving v=8 cm behind the mirror.

**Independent check:** The virtual image is erect and magnification -8/(-24)=1/3.

### Connected

**Question:** The same mirror views an object at x=12 cm. Compare the image with the x=24 cm case.

**Reasoning:** v=12*12/(12+12)=6 cm; the closer object produces an image nearer the pole with larger magnification 1/2.

**Independent check:** v must remain less than f for any finite real object.

### Advanced

**Question:** A student reports v=-7 cm for a real object before a convex mirror under this convention.

**Reasoning:** Reject the sign: behind-mirror virtual images have positive v in the declared Cartesian frame.

**Independent check:** Ray extensions must intersect behind the mirror; substitution with negative v would contradict the model.

## Conditions and limitations

- A virtual image cannot be captured on a screen placed at the apparent image location
- A mirror equation numerical root must be checked against object and image domains
- Mirror sign conventions cannot be silently mixed with unsigned distance formulas

## Diagnose a plausible mistake

**Wrong idea:** A virtual image is the place where reflected light actually converges.

**Diagnostic:** Can a screen behind a convex mirror receive a focused image by placing it at v?

**Repair:** No. Only ray extensions intersect there; the reflected rays are traveling into the object region.

## Exit question

With f=10 cm and x=10 cm, where is the convex-mirror image?

**Answer:** 5 cm behind the mirror. v=fx/(x+f)=100/20=5 cm; magnification is +1/2.
