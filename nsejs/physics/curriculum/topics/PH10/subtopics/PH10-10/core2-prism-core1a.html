Reasoning requirement

Construct the two-reflection heading composition and enforce the stated exit direction, not a memorized prism angle.

Build the scientific model

Use a fixed global heading angle θ (counterclockwise from +x). Reflection in a line whose tangent has heading φ maps θ to 2φ−θ modulo 360°. Normal entry/exit does not turn the ray. Two reflections compose a rotation of twice the signed angle between their reflecting lines. In an isosceles prism with horizontal base and base angle B, the left face tangent is +B, the right face tangent −B. A ray entering left normally has θ₀=B−90°. After reflecting first on right, then left, headings are θ₁=90°−3B and θ₂=5B−90°. Downward normal exit requires θ₂≡270° (mod 360°). The branch must also hit the intended segments in order; the unsilvered face must meet the total-internal-reflection condition if no coating is specified.

Worked teaching anchor

Use a DIFFERENT generic angular example: an incident ray heading 20° strikes a mirror with tangent 60°, giving 100°. A second mirror tangent 140° gives 180°. The outgoing ray points left. Confirm each reflection independently by comparing angles to its own normal.

Misconception and repair

Using one normal for both faces, or setting the outgoing heading equal to the prism base angle without tracking both reflections.

Write one heading transformation per reflecting face; carry the result into the next step and then test direction and aperture.
