Connected textbook foundation (preserved from PR #5)

5. A lens makes refraction work at many points

A thin converging convex lens bends suitable near-axis rays so that a parallel beam tends toward a common focal region. A thin diverging concave lens spreads such rays. The principal axis, optical centre, focal points and focal length are geometric references for drawing the ray system. We use idealized paraxial rays; real lenses can have aberrations, especially far from the axis.

Teach image formation with two independently traced rays, not with a memorized table alone. A ray parallel to the principal axis passes through the appropriate focus after a converging lens; a ray through the optical centre is approximately undeviated in the thin-lens model. Where the real outgoing rays intersect, a real image can be formed on a screen. Where only their backward extensions meet, the image is virtual.

A screen image proves that light actually meets at the screen. A virtual image cannot be projected there by inserting a screen at the apparent location.

Build and check the model

A thin lens combines refraction at two surfaces. In ordinary air a biconvex glass lens is converging and a biconcave glass lens diverging, but power depends on relative index and curvature. For a thin paraxial lens, a ray parallel to the axis emerges toward the far focus (or as if from near focus for a diverger); a ray through the optical centre is approximately undeviated; a ray aimed toward the appropriate focus emerges parallel. These are model rules, not exact for thick or strongly off-axis lenses.

Worked anchor

Converging lens f=10 cm, object 30 cm left. A parallel ray at height h emerges toward (10,0); centre ray from (−30,h) through origin meets it at x=15 cm, height −h/2.

Condition and limiting check

A converging lens in a surrounding medium of equal refractive index has no refracting power in this ideal model; shape alone is insufficient.

Misconception repair

A ray through an optical centre is a thin-lens approximation, not a universal law for all glass elements.
