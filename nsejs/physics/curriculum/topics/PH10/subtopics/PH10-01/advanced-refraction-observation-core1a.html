Reconstructing a medium from independent optical observations

AUTHOR_EXPOSED · ACADEMIC HOLD

Original teaching and transfer; no qualified direct examination source for this construction. Independent QRT and source review HOLD.
Subtopic textbookFresh Core1B

Reasoning goal

Combine independent wavelength and angular observations to identify a consistent optical-index model and reject an ambiguous angle convention.

Scientific construction

For stationary transparent interfaces, frequency is unchanged while speed and wavelength scale as 1/n. Snell's law constrains transmitted direction, independently of the speed/wavelength relation. Use the ratio of wavelength measurements to infer index ratio, then check whether the observed ray is possible and whether the angle is measured from the surface or normal. A normal-incidence direction alone cannot determine index.

Worked anchor

A wave enters from air and wavelength changes from 600 nm to 450 nm, so n₂/n₁=4/3. At normal angle 30°, sin r=0.375, r≈22.0°. A report of r=30° would contradict the measured ratio for an oblique ideal interface.

Common wrong route

Inferring index from bending alone while ignoring wavelength ratio or impossible transmission domain.

Use frequency conservation to get index ratio, then test Snell sine domain and angular reference independently.

Model boundary

What optical observation besides direction could verify index at normal incidence?
