## Learning target and signature
Construct the energy balance for an isolated pair, rather than treating the larger body as a stationary potential source. Select the system and frame; distinguish separation from each body's displacement; use momentum conservation to partition released potential energy. W is the learner's independent decision to account for BOTH kinetic-energy terms and finite initial potential energy.

## Build the warrant
Let two point masses m and n move along their joining line in an inertial centre-of-mass frame, from mutual rest at separation s. Assume Newtonian gravity, no other external work, no collision before the chosen separation r, and negligible radiation. The system potential is U(r) = -Gmn/r (zero at infinity). It belongs to the PAIR, not to each particle individually. Internal forces are equal and opposite, but accelerations need not be equal. Total momentum is zero: m v_m = n v_n for speed magnitudes along opposite directions.
Energy conservation gives
```
K_total = Gmn (1/r - 1/s).
K_total = (1/2)m v_m² + (1/2)n v_n².
```
For equal masses, speeds are equal; K_total = m v², not (1/2)m v² and not 2mv². For unequal masses, insert v_n = (m/n)v_m or use reduced mass μ=mn/(m+n) and relative speed v_rel = v_m + v_n. Then K_total=(1/2)μv_rel². The lighter mass has larger speed and kinetic energy, while momenta have equal magnitudes.

## Worked preparation (not the source question)
Two small spherical bodies of masses m and 2m approach from rest. At a selected separation the decrease in pair potential is E (a positive given). Find individual kinetic energies and speed ratio. Momentum gives v_m=2v_2m. Hence K_m/K_2m=2; K_m=2E/3, K_2m=E/3. Check the energy sum and opposite momentum. This teaches partition without giving the later transfer's separation inference.

## Boundary and misconceptions
If r=s, released energy is zero. For finite s, dropping the initial potential is invalid. As s tends to infinity, the initial potential tends to zero. If one mass is much greater, its recoil tends to zero but the approximation must be justified. In a non-COM laboratory frame, add the centre-of-mass velocity vector; the relative speed is unchanged.
A common wrong route counts the pair potential twice or gives all the released energy to one particle. Repair by writing system-level energy and momentum before using numerical substitutions.
