Vertebrate Immune Architecture: Immunoglobulins, Leukocyte Lineages & B-cell Surface Receptors

National Standard Examination in Junior Science (NSEJS) Reference Item

Problem Statement

[Multiple Correct] Which of the following statements concerning vertebrate immune architecture, antibody structure, and leukocyte lineages are biologically correct?

  • (A) All basic immunoglobulin monomers consist of two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains joined by interchain disulfide bonds, synthesized via the secretory pathway with N-glycosylation.
  • (B) Mature, naive B lymphocytes display both membrane-bound IgM and IgD antigen receptors with identical antigen-binding specificities prior to encounter with antigen.
  • (C) Eosinophils, neutrophils, and basophils differentiate from common myeloid progenitors (CMP) in bone marrow hematopoiesis, while cytotoxic T cells and B cells derive from common lymphoid progenitors (CLP).
  • (D) Plasma cells differ from naive B cells in that plasma cells secrete large amounts of soluble antibodies but lack surface BCR expression.

Select Options & Commit Unassisted Attempt:

Step-by-Step Biological Derivation & Lineage Verification

  1. Immunoglobulin Molecular Architecture: Every basic antibody monomer consists of 2 heavy (H) and 2 light (L) chains linked by interchain disulfide bridges (-S-S-). Antibodies are synthesized via rough ER and Golgi, where they receive N-linked oligosaccharides (glycosylation). (Statement A is true).
  2. Dual Receptor Expression in Naive B Cells: Prior to antigen encounter, mature naive B cells in secondary lymphoid organs co-express membrane IgM (mIgM) and membrane IgD (mIgD). This occurs via alternative pre-mRNA splicing of the primary VDJ-Cμ-Cδ transcript, preserving identical variable domain antigen specificity. (Statement B is true).
  3. Hematopoietic Lineage Differentiation: In bone marrow hematopoiesis, pluripotential stem cells split into common myeloid progenitors (CMP) giving rise to granulocytes (neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils), erythrocytes, and megakaryocytes; and common lymphoid progenitors (CLP) giving rise to T cells, B cells, and NK cells. (Statement C is true).
  4. Plasma Cell Differentiation: Terminal differentiation of activated B cells into plasma cells involves switching from membrane-bound to secretable antibody isoforms via differential RNA polyadenylation, downregulating surface BCRs. (Statement D is true).
✓ Academic Key Verdict: All four statements (A), (B), (C), and (D) are correct.

Core1A — taught operation

Integrating molecular biochemistry (antibody glycosylation), hematological differentiation (myeloid granulocytes vs lymphoid agranulocytes), histological defenses (mucociliary clearance), and B-cell maturation (surface IgM and IgD co-expression).

Justified steps

  1. RETRIEVE — Examine the biochemical structure of immunoglobulins, identifying polypeptide chains and glycosylation.. Why valid: All antibodies are synthesized through the secretory pathway with N-glycosylation.
  2. REPRESENT — Map hematopoietic stem cell differentiation into myeloid vs lymphoid progenitor lineages.. Why valid: Eosinophils arise from common myeloid progenitors (CMP), while lymphocytes arise from common lymphoid progenitors (CLP).
  3. MODEL — Detail innate epithelial barrier mechanisms including mucosal secretion and ciliary beating.. Why valid: Mucus traps foreign particles and directional ciliary strokes propel them out of the respiratory tree.
  4. SYNTHESIZE — Distinguish membrane IgM/IgD co-expression in mature naive B cells from the secretion of soluble antibodies by plasma cells.. Why valid: Mature naive B cells can co-express surface IgM and IgD B-cell receptors via alternative heavy-chain RNA processing; this does not mean they are plasma cells.

Contrast the developmental origin and immune roles of cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) versus neutrophil granulocytes.

  1. 1. Lineage origin: CTLs derive from common lymphoid progenitors and mature in the thymus; neutrophils derive from common myeloid progenitors in bone marrow.
  2. 2. Recognition: CTLs recognize specific viral peptides on MHC Class I via T-cell receptors (adaptive); neutrophils recognize conserved pathogen patterns via PRRs (innate).
  3. 3. Effector mechanism: CTLs release perforin and granzymes to induce apoptosis; neutrophils perform rapid phagocytosis, oxidative burst, and NETosis.
  4. 4. Classification: CTLs are agranular lymphocytes; neutrophils are polymorphonuclear granulocytes.

Misconceptions

  • —
  • Loss of memory B cells necessarily removes neutrophil phagocytosis and every innate barrier. — B-cell memory belongs to humoral adaptive immunity; neutrophils and physical barriers are separately maintained in the stated model.
  • Eosinophils engulf intact helminth worms to clear infection. — Large helminths cannot be ingested whole; eosinophils can participate in extracellular degranulation and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity. This does not make them lymphocytes.
  • Mature naive B cells cannot express both membrane IgM and IgD antigen receptors. — Mature naive human B cells can co-express surface IgM and IgD, whereas plasma-cell antibody secretion is a distinct differentiation state.
