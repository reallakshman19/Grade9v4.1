Microbiology & Virology — Capsid, Envelope & Baltimore Genomes

NSEJS 2024 Reference Item

Problem Statement

[Multiple Correct] Which of the following statements concerning viral structure, molecular genetics, and replication are biologically incorrect?

  • (A) Mature virions are obligate intracellular infectious particles.
  • (B) Viral replication depends strictly on host ribosomes and translational machinery.
  • (C) The viral envelope consists exclusively of host-derived glycolipids without proteins.
  • (D) All animal viruses contain double-stranded DNA genomes.

Select the Biologically INCORRECT Statements:


Biological Science Derivation & Proof

1. System Representation & Setup: Statements A and B describe canonical viral characteristics: mature virions possess no cellular metabolism and are obligate intracellular parasites (A is true); viruses lack ribosomes and tRNAs, relying 100% on host translation (B is true).

2. Physical Model & Formulation: Statement C claims the envelope consists exclusively of host glycolipids without proteins. This is biologically false: during budding, the host-derived lipid bilayer is studded with viral-encoded transmembrane glycoproteins (spikes/peplomers) essential for cell attachment and fusion.

3. Execution & Resolution: Statement D claims all animal viruses contain dsDNA genomes. This is biologically false: animal viruses demonstrate extraordinary genomic diversity in the Baltimore classification, including positive-sense ssRNA (coronaviruses, polio), negative-sense ssRNA (influenza, rabies), retroviruses (HIV), and ssDNA (parvoviruses). Since the prompt asks for biologically incorrect statements, Options C and D are the correct answers.

4. Limiting Check: A and B are eliminated because they are biologically true.

Core 1A: Declarative Teaching Construction

Prerequisite

Chimeric Envelope & Baltimore Classification

Envelopes are acquired from host membranes but must carry viral glycoprotein spikes to bind receptors. Animal viral genomes range from dsDNA to ssDNA, dsRNA, and positive/negative ssRNA.

Worked Example

Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)

HIV buds from host plasma membrane but incorporates viral gp120/gp41 spikes, and its genome consists of two (+)ssRNA molecules.

Independent Exit Check

Rabies & Influenza Virus Check

Both are enveloped (-)ssRNA animal viruses, proving animal viruses are not exclusively dsDNA.
