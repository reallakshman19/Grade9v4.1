## 5. Corrected teaching design — proposed, not implemented
Teach a forward known-interface example disjoint from the inverse challenge: a vertical sealed guide-constrained prism A=50 cm², H=40 cm, upper thickness 20 cm, densities 600 and 1000 kg/m³. Mass at bottom touching interface =0.60 kg; at top flush =1.60 kg; fully lower maximum=2.00 kg. Compare:
- 0.40 kg: upper-only immersed depth 13⅓ cm.
- 1.10 kg: crosses interface, top still exposed; bottom depth 30 cm (upper 20 cm + lower 10 cm).
- 1.80 kg: fully immersed, top 10 cm below surface (upper 10 cm + lower 30 cm).
- 2.10 kg: no unsupported static equilibrium, even fully lower.
Draw the four states with the same height scale and distinct labels; compute displaced mass and upward force from each. A continuation of the partially emerged equation for 1.80 kg gives bottom depth 44 cm > H=40 cm, hence invalid. This teaches the regime switch without revealing the reserved challenge's unknown d, mass thresholds, loaded top or boundary answer. Use a separate *new* inverse task if independent B4 review determines too much overlap; preserve existing revision in history.

Interactive model: user changes load in this guided known-interface example, sees actual immersed lengths, displaced masses and transition thresholds. Plot displaced mass versus bottom depth, with slope changes; mark no-equilibrium case explicitly. The curve is a tool for predicting which equation applies, not a decorative slider. Teach from concrete finite-length volumes, then symbolic relations. End with a genuinely different check (e.g. same body in altered-density layer) before independent challenge.
