# C12 — Gases and Chemical Analysis
## Core1A textbook chapter · original authored learning material · ACADEMIC HOLD

**Curriculum boundary:** foundational gas behaviour, gas observations, combustion, measurement and experiments connect to Class IX–X science. **C12.02 is EXTENSION and C12.05 is ENRICHMENT**; no advanced eudiometric volume algebra is claimed to be compulsory NSEJS Chemistry. Exact lower-class prerequisite concept pins and independent scientific review are pending.

A gas occupies a volume but a measured gas *sample* is not necessarily one chemical species. This chapter begins with the nature of the measured system, then connects conditions and reaction ratios to collection apparatus, condensation, and finally an explicitly advanced reconstruction of gas composition. A learner must learn to separate what was **generated chemically**, what **remained in gas phase**, and what the **instrument collected**.

## C12.01 — Pressure, volume, temperature and controlled comparison · CORE_CANDIDATE

### The observation

A flexible balloon expands in warm water, but the same gas in a rigid sealed container may increase pressure instead. Why should the word 'heated' be insufficient to predict the measured outcome?

### Build the scientific model

A gas consists of widely spaced particles whose collisions with the container produce pressure. Temperature affects typical kinetic energy and collision behaviour; the measured effect depends on which variable is constrained. A rigid sealed vessel holds volume and amount approximately fixed, so heating tends to raise pressure. A flexible vessel can expand, changing volume and possibly pressure. Without stating constraints, a prediction is not a scientific gas model.

At foundational level, discuss compression, expansion, temperature and pressure qualitatively. Formal ideal gas proportionalities and P–V–T calculations are additional models with assumptions: dilute gas, stable amount, negligible condensation, near-ideal behaviour and use of absolute temperature. Do not use Celsius directly as a proportional absolute temperature. When gas is collected over water, water vapour complicates the species inventory; that belongs to a later section.

Construct a two-column apparatus representation: piston-syringe with mobile boundary and rigid flask with fixed boundary. For each state, annotate amount of gas n, pressure P, volume V and absolute temperature T, marking what changed and what is controlled. A graph without correct axes and fixed-variable labels can encode a false relationship. At D4, a student must detect that two 'contradictory' experiments used different boundary conditions.

### Worked scientific reconstruction

A sample of gas at a fixed amount is warmed from 300 K to 330 K inside a freely moving, low-friction piston at approximately constant external pressure. Under an idealised approximation V₂/V₁ = 330/300 = 1.10. The new volume is about 10% greater. If instead the container is rigid, the volume is unchanged and pressure, not volume, would rise by approximately that factor under the same ideal conditions. These quantitative relations are extensions; the school-level conceptual skill is deciding the correct constraint.

### Wrong path and repair

Wrong path: 'Heating any gas always increases its volume.' Repair: identify whether the boundary is rigid, flexible or externally controlled before selecting a model.

### Changed-condition test

Condensation, leaks or chemical gas generation invalidate the fixed-amount assumption. Do not apply a gas law blindly when the chemical identity or amount changes.

### Three distinct independent demands

1. **REPRESENT, provisional D3** — Represent a heated gas in a rigid flask and in a freely moving piston, labelling fixed and changing quantities.
2. **MODEL, provisional D3** — Choose a model for a balloon heated at near-constant outside pressure and for a rigid cylinder; defend why one model does not fit both.
3. **SYNTHESIZE, provisional D3** — Two reports disagree: experiment A heats a sealed rigid gas jar and finds no volume rise; experiment B finds gas volume increased. Reconcile the reports without inventing error.

## C12.02 — Reaction-volume ratios and chemical identities (EXTENSION) · EXTENSION (noncompulsory)

### The observation

Why can two equal volumes of different gases contain comparable particle numbers when temperature and pressure match, yet the reaction products occupy a completely different volume after cooling?

### Build the scientific model

Under the ideal-gas approximation, equal volumes at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of particles for comparable gas mixtures. This underlies the use of gaseous volume ratios from balanced reaction coefficients when all reacting and measured species remain gases at the same reference T/P. For example, 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(g) gives two gas-volume units H₂ and one O₂ forming two units of water vapour at comparable conditions. If water condenses, the measured gas volume is no longer two units.

Reaction-volume analysis requires a species ledger. Start with the amount of each gas; consume reactants according to the balanced equation; produce gases or vapours, then change phase separately. Excess reactant remains and must be included in the final measured volume. The temperature, pressure, gas solubility and wetness of the measurement apparatus can invalidate a naive volume contraction calculation.

The numerical laws of gas reaction volumes are an **EXTENSION**, not an excuse to promote formal senior-secondary gas-law content into the mandatory Class X syllabus. D3 tasks should use a few clear integer ratios under controlled conditions. D4 tasks should introduce an exception—cooling, dissolution or excess reagent—and demand the correct stage model rather than a memorised formula.

### Worked scientific reconstruction

Start with 2.0 volume units of H₂ and 1.0 unit of O₂ at a common T/P, ignite safely only in an authorised demonstration apparatus (never an unsupervised home experiment), and assume complete reaction. The chemical reaction consumes both exactly. At a reference temperature high enough for water vapour to remain gaseous, 2.0 units of H₂O(g) result. Cool sufficiently to condense water, and the dry gaseous volume can approach zero. The volume contraction after cooling is not the same as the high-temperature gas-volume change.

### Wrong path and repair

Wrong path: 'Coefficients always equal measured gas-volume ratio.' Repair: enforce common reference T/P, gas phase, complete reaction and absence of excess reactants before using the ratio.

### Changed-condition test

If one gas product dissolves into the collection liquid, the observed volume can be smaller for a reason unrelated to chemical stoichiometric contraction.

### Three distinct independent demands

1. **REPRESENT, provisional D3** — Represent 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O before reaction, as hot vapour and after complete condensation.
2. **MODEL, provisional D3** — A gas mixture contains excess hydrogen and some oxygen. Choose a reaction-volume ledger that predicts the residue after cooling.
3. **SYNTHESIZE, provisional D3** — A student infers gas composition from one final volume without recording temperature and product phase. Explain why that inference is underdetermined.

## C12.03 — Gas collection apparatus and measurement uncertainty · CORE_CANDIDATE

### The observation

Gas produced in a reaction is collected over water. The graduated cylinder shows 50 mL, so a student writes '50 mL pure dry carbon dioxide'. What hidden assumptions are being made?

### Build the scientific model

Gas collection by water displacement is convenient for relatively insoluble gases, but the collection may include water vapour. A gas soluble in water can partly dissolve instead of appearing in the graduated gas space; carbon dioxide has appreciable solubility and can react with water. If the water levels inside and outside the vessel differ, the gas pressure also differs from ambient pressure. A leakage path can reduce the collected amount without changing chemical formation.

A gas syringe avoids direct dissolution into a water trough but introduces its own problems: dead volume, friction, small leaks and temperature changes. The method choice must fit the actual gas and experiment. An appropriate diagram labels the reaction flask, gas outlet, water/trough or syringe, collection pressure and measurement scale. Do not infer gas identity from the existence of bubbles or from a numerical volume reading.

Measurement uncertainty is not a nuisance to be deleted after calculation. Record what was measured, units, instrument range, uncertainty and whether the sample is wet. At D3, explain the difference between generated and collected gas; at D4, use controls to diagnose competing systematic biases. A closed apparatus with clear pressure and temperature measurements supports stronger claims than a single unchecked meniscus reading.

### Worked scientific reconstruction

A carbonate reaction may chemically generate 100 mL of CO₂ at a chosen standard reference condition. If only 85 mL is collected over water, the 15 mL difference does not show conservation failed. Some CO₂ may dissolve, some may leak, and measured wet-gas T/P may not match the reference. To identify the dominant error, collect using an appropriate alternative, calibrate pressure/temperature and separately assess water solubility. Never compensate with an arbitrary 15% factor.

### Wrong path and repair

Wrong path: 'Displaced water volume is always the exact volume of dry pure gas produced.' Repair by checking gas composition, vapour, dissolution, pressure equality and leaks.

### Changed-condition test

Collection of soluble or reactive gases over water is especially unreliable. Choose a chemically suitable collection method rather than automatically applying an apparatus diagram.

### Three distinct independent demands

1. **REPRESENT, provisional D3** — Represent water displacement for collected gas and label the difference between generated gas and measured wet gas.
2. **MODEL, provisional D3** — A water-soluble gas is being generated. Select between water displacement and gas syringe collection and justify the controls.
3. **SYNTHESIZE, provisional D3** — Two apparatus report different collected CO₂ volumes for the same reaction. Synthesize an error-analysis procedure before assigning stoichiometric failure.

## C12.04 — Combustion products, condensation and residual gases · CORE_CANDIDATE

### The observation

A hydrocarbon burns in excess oxygen and the hot gas is cooled. The total gas volume drops even though no carbon atom has vanished. Which stage accounts for the loss of measured gas?

### Build the scientific model

For complete combustion of a hydrocarbon containing only carbon and hydrogen, the usual products are carbon dioxide and water. The general balanced formula for CₓHᵧ under sufficient oxygen is CₓHᵧ + (x + y/4)O₂ → xCO₂ + (y/2)H₂O. This equation conserves atoms, but the gas state of H₂O depends on temperature. High-temperature water vapour can condense when cooled, removing water molecules from the measured gas phase without removing them from the closed material system.

Excess oxygen survives in the residual gas. Carbon dioxide may be absorbed later by a suitable reagent or partly dissolved in water; each operation must be treated as a separate stage. If insufficient oxygen is supplied, carbon monoxide and soot can form; the simple complete-combustion equation is no longer the full description. Distinguish products expected from an idealised chemical model from those actually established by product tests.

Plot a species inventory at three points: before ignition; after combustion while all idealised products remain in gas phase; after cooling and condensation. Write the conserved carbon, hydrogen and oxygen totals for the entire material system, then count only species remaining in the **gas** phase. D3 tasks translate between formulas and volumes; D4 tasks detect mistaken conflation of chemical conversion with physical removal from gas space.

### Worked scientific reconstruction

Burn methane in a simplified idealised chamber with 1 mol CH₄ and 3 mol O₂. Complete reaction CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O consumes 2 mol O₂ and leaves 1 mol. At high enough temperature the gas inventory is CO₂ 1, H₂O(v) 2 and O₂ 1 = 4 mol gases. After cooling so water condenses, the dry gas contains CO₂ 1 + O₂ 1 = 2 mol. The reduction of gas amount is 2 mol due to condensation; the atoms are still present in liquid water.

### Wrong path and repair

Wrong path: 'Gas volume contraction is entirely the amount of CO₂ that formed.' Repair by separating chemical reaction, condensation and selective absorption.

### Changed-condition test

If the water does not fully condense or CO₂ dissolves, the measured 'dry gas' account is different. Experimental phase and collection conditions must be recorded.

### Three distinct independent demands

1. **REPRESENT, provisional D3** — Represent methane combustion inventory before heating, while water is vapour, and after cooling, assuming excess O₂.
2. **MODEL, provisional D3** — A gas volume decreases after cooling combustion exhaust. Choose a model distinguishing condensation from absorption of CO₂.
3. **SYNTHESIZE, provisional D3** — Residual gas after complete hydrocarbon combustion contains CO₂ and O₂. Construct a stage ledger to show which volume loss diagnoses each species.

## C12.05 — Eudiometry: reconstructing composition from stages (ENRICHMENT) · ENRICHMENT (noncompulsory)

### The observation

An unknown gas mixture burns, cools, then passes through a selective absorber. A single number called 'contraction' appears on the instrument. Can it reveal a complete molecular formula by itself?

### Build the scientific model

Eudiometry is a multi-stage gas-volume analysis, generally beyond ordinary Class X Chemistry and therefore marked **ENRICHMENT**. A typical process combines a known gas with oxygen, burns it in a controlled apparatus, cools to a chosen reference condition, selectively absorbs a product such as CO₂ and measures residual volumes. The inference depends on knowing what each stage removes. A gas-volume drop after cooling may include water condensation, while a later reduction with suitable CO₂ absorber is attributed to carbon dioxide under validated selectivity.

To analyse a hydrocarbon CₓHᵧ, use the complete-combustion equation and amount ledger. At consistent T/P, CO₂ produced is proportional to x times hydrocarbon amount, while O₂ consumed is proportional to (x + y/4). A single contraction value may not determine both x and y; an independent measurement of CO₂, O₂ consumption or initial hydrocarbon volume can provide further constraints. When an absorber's chemistry is not selective, the problem may remain underdetermined.

Build a table with rows *initial*, *after combustion*, *after cooling* and *after absorption*. Columns list hydrocarbon, O₂, CO₂, H₂O vapour and any inert gas. Never jump from the first to the final volume without accounting for intermediate water and excess oxygen. The apparatus must be safe and sealed: explosive gas experiments are not for unsupervised learners; present a conceptual or teacher-controlled simulation.

### Worked scientific reconstruction

Consider 10 mL methane mixed with 30 mL oxygen, all at a common reference T/P and ideal complete reaction. CH₄ consumes 20 mL O₂ and produces 10 mL CO₂ and 20 mL water vapour at matched gaseous conditions. After cooling and full condensation, residual dry gases are 10 mL CO₂ + 10 mL excess O₂ = 20 mL. A selective CO₂ absorber leaves 10 mL oxygen. The loss during absorption is 10 mL; that cannot be conflated with the 20 mL contraction from initial 40 mL to cooled 20 mL.

### Wrong path and repair

Wrong path: 'Contraction alone proves the hydrocarbon formula.' Repair with complete species ledger, validated absorption selectivity and independent observations.

### Changed-condition test

An inert gas contaminant or partially dissolved CO₂ changes residual volumes. Identify what the absorber does not remove and whether the gas is compared at the same T/P.

### Three distinct independent demands

1. **REPRESENT, provisional D3** — Represent each stage of the example 10 mL CH₄ + 30 mL O₂ after reaction, cooling and CO₂ absorption.
2. **MODEL, provisional D3** — An unknown gas reacts in oxygen and the cooled gas contracts by 18 mL, with no separate CO₂ reading. Choose a model and state why composition may be underdetermined.
3. **SYNTHESIZE, provisional D3** — A record gives three volume drops but fails to state whether temperature/pressure are matched. Synthesize a valid correction and evidence request.

## Chapter synthesis

The key distinction is **chemical material inventory versus gas-phase inventory versus measured instrument volume**. Always record species, pressure, temperature, water vapour, solubility, collection losses and stage order before claiming a molecular formula. The additional eudiometry treatment is an openly declared enrichment whose gas-reaction and adsorption assumptions demand further scientific qualification. Do not equate a mathematically neat volume contraction with a verified NSEJS source question.
